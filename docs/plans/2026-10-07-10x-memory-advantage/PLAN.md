## 0. The plan in one page

**The brief says accuracy is saturated and a 10x must come from fewer wrong answers, fewer tokens, cheaper and bigger brains, or capabilities nobody else has. The audits agree with the direction and correct five premises; the CEO reviews correct the corrections.** Each one changes what we build or how we measure it:

1. **The sealed LoCoMo set is nearly spent, and one more opening is queued.** Its 7 sealed conversations have been opened three times (P2 E2, P3 E1, P6); the open-source shootout's Phase 7 is a fourth opening that has not run yet (its cells are in `manifests/cells/sealed.json` on #89 head `9c07b7e`, approved by the maintainer on 2026-10-07, "runs after Phases 4 to 6"). The split file calls the set "diagnostic evidence, never a primary confirmation". Bet 1's confirmation moves to sealed-confirmation v2 (200 questions, 2 of 3 openings left). Whether Phase 7 waits for the gbrain adapter fix is the maintainer's call (section 10, challenge 1); the hold request has been sent to GBRA-1.
2. **"22k vs 7k" compares different tokenizers, and the matched gap is not known.** gbrain's 22,242 is Claude tokens for text that is 13,794 GPT tokens; the 7k figure for `ext-extract-first` is self-reported with no tokenizer, so no matched external ratio can be computed from it (the "about 2x" in the first draft was an unverified cross-report number). In the matched shootout `ext-extract-first`'s default context is about 1,262 tokens and `ext-memory-bank` wins at 8,739 [recount]. Every deterministic way we have tried to deliver less text has lost accuracy: chunks 132 vs whole conversations 192 of 200 (sealed), neighbor windows closed 30 to 36% of the gap, the lexical excerpt selector 48 vs 53 of 60. Those results reject those selectors; they do not prove that a model-written brief is necessary. So wave 1 is a pilot that makes the brief compete against cheaper designs (a cheap model reading the intact evidence directly, a write-time digest, a cached raw-evidence control), and the headline counts the builder's tokens.
3. **In the matched 8k head-to-head, gbrain's harness adapter misstates it, and fixing the adapter makes gbrain honest, not first** [recount]: 0.59 with its own chunk items; 0.78 when every system's items are rehydrated to the sessions they cite (first among rehydrated arms, 2 points ahead on n = 100). But `ext-memory-bank`'s native 8k arm scores 0.89 at 8,739 tokens and `ext-graph-pipeline` 0.83, both above gbrain's best case; the 0.979 strict recall ties `ext-memory-bank`; and on LoCoMo dev gbrain rehydrated (0.727) sits below `ext-extract-first` (0.760) and `ext-memory-bank` (0.756). Rehydration is a component diagnostic that removes part of a competitor's product; the purchasing comparison is native whole-system frontiers. The adapter returns `hybridSearch` chunk text instead of the shipped whole-conversation delivery. Fixing that arm is cheap and must precede any new comparator data, and its expected result (about 0.78 on LongMemEval-S, still behind two systems) is preregistered so nobody is surprised.
4. **BEAM-1M's 53.5% needs its floor, and the floor is not measured yet** [recount]: 49 of 194 answerable questions need more than 5 gold turn groups and can never pass strict recall@5, which is a retrieval-feasibility fact and is reported separately from the answer rubric; the 0.278 no-memory score is BEAM-100K's (120 questions), and the 1M floor is unmeasured; the run used no reranker, OpenAI embeddings, PGLite with about 2.8k chunks per brain, and a `gpt-4.1-mini` reader that the model rules allow only as the bridge to earlier runs, never for a product claim. Fixes wait for a failure analysis, and "53.5%" is never quoted again without its floor and the strict all-items-met rate.
5. **"7 core verbs + discover" reproduces the arm that lost.** P8's sealed surface test lost 9.9 points pooled with the `verbs` surface and 8.5 with `starter`, with 50 points lost on the 20 hidden-tool tasks, and saved no money ($236.75 full vs $233.56 starter). The discover call models made sent `tools: []`, which returns empty by design. The empty-discovery fix, grants that can write in bulk and `gbrain setup` ship regardless of the surface question. Today three different defaults ship at once: README and `docs/mcp/*` register `--surface verbs`, the two coding-agent plugin manifests and `REGISTRATION_SURFACE` pin `starter`, and OpenClaw, Hermes and the thin CLI get `full`; wave 0's S0 aligns them on one named constant. Whether the advertised surface narrows at all, and to what, is the maintainer's call (section 10, challenge 3).

Four more facts shape the waves. Zero-LLM writes are real against the extraction-heavy systems (ingest $1.53 against $32 to $109 for `ext-memory-bank`, `ext-extract-first`, `ext-graph-pipeline` and `ext-temporal-graph` on the same 100 histories [recount]) but **two cheap systems already cost less than gbrain** (`ext-markdown-kb` $1.45, the plain hybrid control $1.28 [recount]) and **the shipped default is not zero-LLM**: background fact extraction costs $9.94 per 1,000 pages against $0.32 with it off (P8 sealed), and $9.94 per 1,000 pages sits inside the $6.7 to $12.7 per 1,000 sessions the three extraction comparators cost on the shootout data [recount; the units differ and the cost table carries both]. The Q1 scoreboard freezes `gbrain-defaults` as its headline row on Oct 13 to 15, so the write-path default is decided before then (section 10, challenge 2), with a current cheap extraction model as the third option. **At company scale gbrain trails plain files**: −16 points at 52,028 documents (Cat 40 scale tier) and −11.3 at 55,235 (Cat 40 Hard, #76), so "gbrain should look best at the high end" is a premise to test, and the production scale target is the flagship brain's real size (about 1.28M chunks derived from #6223, not verified; wave 0 measures it). **Recorded-time history** ("what did the brain have recorded on March 1") exists in no op and does not survive a Markdown rebuild; `ext-temporal-graph` ships bi-temporal edges, so recorded time is parity, and the unique part is Markdown-durable, holder-attributed history with reviewable diffs. And **nobody has measured whether automatic take grading is right**; takes extraction precision is under 0.80 for every model on provisional, incomplete labels, so a public "who has been right" board stays deferred (section 10, challenge 6).

**The program-level primary** (section 3) is the one the 2026-09-28 plan's amendment 2 bound: a tenfold reduction in end-to-end failures on a fixed set of memory-dependent agent tasks against the frozen release, under fixed latency, token and cost limits. The bet-level metrics below are secondary mechanisms, not interchangeable 10x claims.

## 3. The 10x contract

### 3.1 The program-level primary (binding)

The 2026-09-28 plan's amendment 2 binds "10x" to one preregistered outcome: a tenfold reduction in end-to-end failures on a fixed set of memory-dependent agent tasks, compared with a frozen previous release, within fixed latency, token and cost limits, and with no violation of the critical safety contracts. This plan inherits that primary unchanged. The frozen release is gbrain v0.60.106.0 (`7aa2caa0`), the scope pin.

The candidate workload is cross-session meeting or reply preparation after a correction: session 1 records a commitment, a dated change and a correction; session 2 asks the agent to prepare for the meeting or draft the reply without naming the fact. A failure is an unsupported or stale answer or action, or a missed commitment, not a missing benchmark point. The wave 5 family P tasks (D6) and the Cat 41 scenarios (D7) are the natural carriers; wave 1 freezes the workload before anything else is measured.

Frozen before the pilot, in a committed preregistration: the target user (an engineer or founder running an agent harness against a personal brain), the task distribution, the maximum acceptable extra failures (the loss tolerance), and the total resource envelope (latency, tokens, dollars). Insufficient power changes the sample size or yields "inconclusive"; it never widens the loss tolerance. The measured factor is published whatever it is, including 1.2x.

### 3.2 Bet-level metrics (secondary mechanisms)

The bets below are mechanisms that could move the primary. Each is preregistered with its baseline from committed data so "the measured factor was 3x" is a publishable outcome, but none of them is the 10x claim. Exact correctness and safety assertions fail immediately and never need significance; noisy quality metrics use non-inferiority with a tolerance frozen before the data is seen; dashboards gate nothing.

### Wave 1: The decision pilot

Goal: freeze the program primary and baseline it on the frozen release, then run one bounded architecture pilot that decides whether a model-written evidence brief earns a new paid operation, which design wins at a matched budget, and in what order waves 2 to 6 run. ## 5. What we stop doing

- Chasing LongMemEval-S points. It is a development and regression set from here on.
- Adding model calls to the write path by default without the maintainer's decision. Any new write-time model call (the DIGEST arm included) ships opt-in with its cost in the table. The existing default (fact extraction on, Sonnet 4.6) is decided by the maintainer before Q1's freeze (section 10, challenge 2), not changed quietly and not kept quietly either.
- Adding advertised tools. New capabilities in this plan are parameters on existing ops (`known_as_of`, `beliefs`, `intent`, `counterparty`, `group_by`, `mode`) wherever an existing op's contract fits. Under D4's advertised `full`, every new op is advertised (`surface.ts:217-228` returns every operation for `full`), so nothing in this plan is called "hidden" unless the approved surface outcome actually hides it. The complete list of new ops, each with its surface membership under every challenge 3 outcome and its character cost against the 25,000-character `starter` budget:
  - A9: preferred form `assemble_evidence {mode: 'brief'}` (a parameter, no new name); fallback a read-scope `evidence_brief` op in `full` only, about 600 characters, justified and measured per its row.
  - W7: `takes_scorecard {group_by}` (a parameter); no `track_record` op.
  - W9: one read op for proposals (`list` and `show --diff`) and one write op for `accept`, `reject` and `undo`, both in `full` only, the write op behind the explicitly authorized owner-admin preview/apply path (admin scope on a hosted connection, or the local CLI), not a "trusted-local" gate that stdio cannot satisfy, about 900 characters together; named in W9's PR before it opens.
  - B9: none; it reads E-C's `projection_readiness` fields.
  - `discover`: only if challenge 3 keeps a narrowed surface; then it is specified as an advertised alias of `request_tools` (same handler, old callers unchanged) and the PR states whether both names consume schema budget.
  Every new op or parameter gets keyless, opt-out and budget-exhaustion agent cases that verify the chosen call and its fallback, not only schema validity.
- Quoting BEAM numbers without their no-memory floor and the strict all-items-met rate, or quoting the 100K floor as the 1M floor.
- Shipping deterministic compression of evidence (excerpt selectors, windows, more chunks at the same budget) as a default. Every one has lost. A narrow, mechanically different alternative may still compete in the wave 1 pilot; the pilot, not the ban, decides.
- Reusing opened sealed sets as if they were held out.
- Calling a rehydrated arm a product result, or an unverified cross-report ratio a matched one.
