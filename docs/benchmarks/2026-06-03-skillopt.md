# Can rewriting an agent's instructions improve its work?

**Historical run: June 3, 2026.** gbrain v0.42.9.0; fictional `skillopt-v1` corpus. Target model: Claude Haiku 4.5. Rewrite proposals: Claude Sonnet 4.6.

A skill is a Markdown file telling an agent how to do a job. `skillopt` proposes edits to that file and evaluates them. In these experiments, four deliberately deficient skills improved on tasks with different topics. A one-shot rewrite also solved the simple example, so this report does not show that an iterative optimizer was necessary.

**Evidence limitation:** the original receipts were not copied from the ignored `eval/reports/` directory before cleanup. The figures below are historical reported results. Moreover, training and held-out tasks originally shared identical scoring rules. The August 31 fixture update introduced different held-out judge criteria; these older numbers establish, at most, transfer to new topics under the same rules.

## The four starting flaws

| Skill | The flaw |
|---|---|
| brief-writer | never includes a risks section or a confidence level |
| thorough-analyst | rambles; no length discipline |
| quick-answerer | answers from memory; never checks the brain |
| advisor | discusses endlessly; never commits to a recommendation |

The optimizer saw training tasks. The harness scored separate tasks before and after the rewrite, rather than trusting the optimizer's own success report. A held-out task is one withheld during tuning; this reduces one route to memorizing examples, but it does not by itself rule out learning shortcuts in a shared rubric.

## Did the instructions improve? Category 30

| Skill | Held-out before | Held-out after |
|---|---|---|
| brief-writer (missing structure) | 0.00 | **1.00** |
| thorough-analyst (too verbose) | 0.00 | **1.00** |
| quick-answerer (no brain-first) | 0.00 | **1.00** |
| advisor (no verdict) | 0.00 | **1.00** |

All four reported scores went from 0.00 to 1.00. The historical gate passed, at reported cost $4.95. That is evidence that clear defects in instructions can be repaired. These tiny, single-flaw skills are not a measurement of improvements to large production skills.

## Could a rewrite exploit the scorer? Category 32

A deliberately weak scorer checked only whether section headings appeared. A hand-written cheating skill produced headings without useful content: it scored 1.00 on that check and 0.28 on an independent quality judge, a 0.72 gap.

In the optimizer run with a separate validation check, reported held-out quality rose from 0.22 to 0.48 while the easy score reached its maximum. The gate passed at $1.63. This shows the independent judge caught one known shortcut; it does not establish that the optimizer cannot cheat or that the judge is impossible to fool.

## Did a rewrite help another model? Category 33

| Optimized on | Evaluated on | Unoptimized | After transfer | Native ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Haiku | Sonnet | 0.00 | **1.00** | 1.00 |
| Sonnet | Haiku | 0.00 | **0.71** | 0.96 |

Both tested transfers improved over the unoptimized skill. The Sonnet-to-Haiku transfer reached 0.71 against a native 0.96 result, about 74% of that measured benefit. The gate passed at $9.22. These two cases support testing reusable instructions across models; they do not prove transfer to every model or task.

## Was the full loop worth its cost? Category 31

| Variant | Held-out | Training-set | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| no optimization (floor) | 0.00 | — | — |
| **A — full loop + validation gate** | **1.00** | 1.00 | $0.98 |
| B — single-reflect loop | 0.92 | 1.00 | $0.94 |
| C — one-shot rewrite (no loop, no gate) | 1.00 | 1.00 | $0.88 |
| D — greedy, no validation gate | 0.92 | 0.80 | $0.96 |

The full loop and one-shot rewrite both reached 1.00 on held-out tasks. Single reflection and ungated greedy edits reached 0.92. All four improved over the 0.00 starting point, so the historical gate passed. The costs ranged from $0.88 to $0.98.

The validation gate did not reduce quality relative to the ungated variant in this example. Calling it “free insurance” would overstate one run: it has a cost, and broader failure coverage was not measured. The useful design idea is to check the behavior one cares about independently of the score used to propose edits.

The skills and tasks are in `eval/data/skillopt-v1/`, generated by `eval/generators/skillopt-v1-gen.ts`. The held-out criteria changed on August 31, 2026 after audit finding `skillopt-cats-09`. New numbers must identify that fixture revision.

The first full run reportedly improved 0/4 skills and exposed five implementation problems, including a tool-loop/SDK incompatibility, missing pricing that stopped cost-capped work, a swallowed error, and the optimizer not being told what the scorer expected. Fixing the execution path and exposing training criteria preceded the successful run. That history explains why testing the whole feature against models was useful, without treating the later pass as proof of general safety.
