# Briefing Skill

Compile a daily briefing from brain context, correct **as of the briefing date** the user gives.

> **Filing rule:** When the briefing creates or updates brain pages,
> follow `skills/_brain-filing-rules.md`.

## Contract

- Every fact includes an inline `[Source: slug, updated DATE]` citation.
- Every fact is true **on the briefing date**. That date may be in the past relative to the brain. Records dated after it are future events and are never the answer.
- Meeting participants are resolved against the brain by exact match only. Gaps are flagged explicitly.
- Active deals and action items include deadlines and recency context.
- The briefing is read-only. No brain pages are created or modified unless the user explicitly asks.
- Sections whose tools are unavailable are named as missing, not shown as empty.

## Step 0: Read the task's output contract first

Tasks often specify how the briefing will be read, for example "one entry per external meeting, starting with time and title exactly as listed", "state the asked-for fact in a sentence of its own", or "write 'No brain page for <name>'". These rules override the default format. Before you finish, check every rule:

1. **One entry per external meeting.**
   - Start each entry with the exact time and title from the calendar. Copy them character for character.
   - Use the order the calendar gives.
   - Internal or personal blocks (standup, focus block, lunch with no attendee, inbox) get a one-line "internal, no prep" note or are grouped. Never interleave them in a way that splits an external entry.
2. **Lead with the answer.**
   - The first sentence after the heading states the requested fact plainly, for the briefing date. Examples: "X is with Company today." / "The list price in force today is USD N per seat per year." / "Company's chief executive today is Name."
   - Cite it in that same sentence.
   - Alternative or historical values go in later sentences, clearly labelled as not in force.
3. **Missing person.** When an attendee has no exact-match page, write the exact phrase `No brain page for <Full Name>`. Say you cannot state their company. Do not guess from near-name matches.
4. Keep everything for a meeting inside that meeting's entry, before the next meeting's heading.

## Efficient retrieval (budget your calls)

Finish the briefing. One past run ran out of time and shipped only part of it. Prefer few, targeted calls:

- **Batch names.** One keyword search with all names OR-ed together (`"A" OR "B" OR "C"`, match: keyword) usually surfaces every person and company page at once. Then use per-name searches only where needed.
- **Contracts.** `list_pages` with `slug_prefix: contracts/<account-slug>` lists the full contract chain: MSA, amendments, rescissions, side letters, renewal orders. Search results alone may miss some of these.
- **Reading pages.** `get_page` on the key page (company, person, or the relevant contracts) when snippets are not enough.
- **Leadership changes.** One "names/appoints chief executive" style search per company. Watch for similarly named companies.
- **Optional pulls.** `get_recent_salience`, `find_anomalies` and `get_backlinks` are optional. After a bulk import they are usually noise. Run at most once each, and never at the expense of the meeting answers.

## Resolving the asked-for fact as of the briefing date

### Page dates are not fact dates

- Many pages carry a fallback or bulk-import "updated" date, often later than the briefing date. Cite the date as recorded; a short note explaining it is fine.
- Decide currency from dates **inside** the content: role start and end dates, signed and effective dates, announcement dates.

### Person: which company are they with today

- Use the person page's role history. Pick the role whose start is on or before the briefing date and whose end (if any) is after it.
- Cross-check against the company or account page.
- Ignore moves that start after the briefing date, and ignore rumours.
- Watch for near-duplicate names: different spellings, same first name, same surname. Only an exact full-name match counts. When a lookalike exists, mention the lookalike as a different person.

### Company: who is its chief executive today

- Use the leadership list and tenure dates. A founder who became executive chair is not the CEO after the handover.
- Do not use the parent company's CEO for a subsidiary.
- Do not use a similarly named different company's announcements.

### Account term: price, seats, discount, support tier, payment, renewal date

1. List every contract for the account and note each document's **effective date** (not just its signed date).
2. Start from the MSA. Apply each amendment or renewal order in effective-date order, but only those effective on or before the briefing date.
3. A rescission cancels its amendment from the rescission's effective date. The prior value comes back.
4. A document that changes other fields leaves the asked-for field untouched. Carry the earlier value forward.
5. A document signed or effective after the briefing date is not in force. Mention it as a later change, not as today's value.
6. **Renewal date** means the last day of the term in force today.
7. Ignore these as sources of the answer, and mention them as unconfirmed if useful:
   - account-page snapshots ("last reviewed …"), which are often stale; the page itself usually says signed contracts govern
   - figures floated in meetings but never signed
   - Slack, hallway or secondhand notes
8. Cite the contract that **set** the value in force. For example, cite the MSA if later documents didn't change the field; cite the amendment that changed it otherwise.

### Useful extra context (after the answer sentence)

- Contacts current today. Note people who have left, or who haven't joined yet.
- The account owner.
- Notice deadline: term end minus the notice period.
- Open follow-ups from past meetings dated on or before today.
- Working-style notes.

Flag stale material, meaning the last dated interaction is more than about a year old.

## Pre-Briefing Context Pull (when tools exist)

Run these only if the tools are available. If a tool is not available, list that section under "Not covered".

- **Salience scan** (`gbrain salience --days 7` / `get_recent_salience`). Fold relevant hits into "High-Salience Pages". If all hits are bulk-import pages with zero emotional weight, say so in one line.
- **Anomalies** (`gbrain anomalies` / `find_anomalies`). Spikes against a zero baseline after a bulk import are not signal.
- **Personal recall** (`gbrain recall --query ...`), for preferences and prior commitments.
- **Hot memory pulse** (`gbrain recall --since-last-run --supersessions --pending --rollup --json`). Order: contradictions resolved, then top mentions, then new facts, then the pending-consolidation footer.
- **Open loops** (`gbrain waiting --json`). If it refuses on a stale source, run the named sync and retry. Never build the "waiting on you" list from stale mail.

## Phases

1. **Today's meetings.** Resolve each external party and answer the asked-for fact (see above).
2. **Active deals.** Terms in force, term end, notice deadlines, anything effective in the next 7 days.
3. **Action items.** Dated open follow-ups, deadlines within 48 hours, overdue items.
4. **Recent changes / People in play.** Only if recency data is meaningful. When pages share a bulk-import timestamp, say these sections can't be ranked reliably.
5. **Stale alerts.** Stale pages relevant to today's meetings, such as old snapshots or old last-contact dates.

## Identity rule

- Find an attendee's page by exact match only: `gbrain whois <email|name> --json`, `resolve_slugs`, or an exact keyword search on the full name. Exactly one match counts.
- A search's top hit is not the attendee.
- Fuzzy or partial-name hits are different people. You may list them as "not this attendee".
- The same rule applies to email senders. No match or an ambiguous match means an unknown sender.

## Output Format

```
DAILY BRIEFING: [weekday, date]
========================
[One short note on dating: facts are as of the briefing date, and page "updated" stamps may be import fallbacks.]

MEETINGS TODAY
- [time] [meeting title exactly as listed]
  [Answer sentence for the asked fact] [Source: slug, updated DATE]
  [Supporting and other values, labelled as not in force] [Source: ...]
  [Contacts, context, cautions about lookalike names or companies]

- [time] [internal meeting]: internal, no prep needed.

ACTIVE DEALS
- [account]: [term end, notice deadline, upcoming effective changes] [Source: ...]

ACTION ITEMS
- [item]: due [date], related to [slug]

HIGH-SALIENCE / ANOMALIES / RECENT CHANGES / PEOPLE IN PLAY
- [hits, or a one-line explanation of why there is no signal]

NOT COVERED
- [sections whose tools were unavailable or not run: missing, not empty]
```

## Citation in Briefings

- Format: `[Source: slug, updated DATE]`, for example "Jane is CTO of Acme [Source: people/jane-doe, updated 2026-04-01]".
- Use the page's slug. For contracts and meetings, the document's own date is acceptable as the DATE.
- Never cite a slug you didn't actually retrieve.

## When it fails

Follow the [agent operator protocol](../conventions/agent-operator-protocol.md) for gbrain errors.

- **`waiting` refuses on stale data:** sync, then retry. Don't use stale mail.
- **Degraded `recall` or `query`:** say "the brain is searching keywords only right now".
- **One source fails** (`rate_limited`, `timeout`): ship the rest of the briefing and name the missing section.
- **Running low on time or calls:** finish every meeting entry first. The asked-for facts are the priority. Then list the uncovered sections.

## Anti-Patterns

- **Answering with a value from a document not yet in force on the briefing date**, or from a rescinded amendment.
- **Treating a page's "updated" stamp as when the fact was true.**
- **Using account snapshots, floated proposals or hearsay as the in-force value.**
- **Matching an attendee to a similar name**, or a company to a similarly named company or its parent.
- **Burying the answer** after background, or putting it under a different meeting's entry.
- **Renaming or reformatting meeting titles** in the heading.
- **Uncited facts.**
- **Modifying brain pages unprompted.**
- **Silently dropping sections.** Name what wasn't covered.

## Tools Used

- search / query: semantic and keyword search; OR-batched exact names
- resolve_slugs, whois: exact identity resolution
- get_page: compiled truth, role history, contract terms
- list_pages: the full contract chain by slug prefix
- get_timeline: dated events
- get_recent_salience, find_anomalies, get_backlinks: optional pulse checks
