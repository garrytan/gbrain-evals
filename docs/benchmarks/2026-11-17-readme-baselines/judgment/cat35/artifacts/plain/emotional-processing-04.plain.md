# Casey Nguyen – DevThink 2025 Rejection & Calendar Defrag Project

## Context
Casey received a conference rejection from DevThink 2025 for a talk titled **"Calendar Defragmentation: Reclaiming Deep Work Hours Through Systematic Meeting Consolidation."** The committee feedback was "leaned more toward product-specific implementation details rather than the theoretical frameworks we emphasize in the main track." Casey processed this late at night and emerged with a concrete plan.

## The Talk / Essay Framework
The core idea: **fragmented calendars are an organizational design failure**, not an individual discipline problem. Meeting placement has measurable cognitive costs modeled through research on context switching.

**Key research anchors:** Gloria Mark (interruption recovery, 23+ minute costs), Mary Czerwinski (task switching), Cal Newport (deep work theory).

**Proposed structure:**
1. The problem and why individual solutions fail
2. Cognitive cost model for meeting placement
3. Meeting taxonomy by focus-destruction potential
4. Consolidation patterns that navigate organizational politics
5. Measurement / proving ROI to leadership

**Velocita Labs result:** Median uninterrupted work block increased from 47 minutes to 2.3 hours over two quarters.

## What Got the Abstract Rejected (Likely)
The final submitted abstract was otherwise theoretically framed, but this sentence probably triggered a "case study / product-specific" categorization on a speed-read:
> *"At Velocita Labs, applying these principles increased our median uninterrupted work block from 47 minutes to 2.3 hours over two quarters."*

The lesson: genre signals matter under skim conditions. The fix for practitioner conferences is to **lead with the metrics**, then use the research as the "why it works" layer rather than the opening anchor.
