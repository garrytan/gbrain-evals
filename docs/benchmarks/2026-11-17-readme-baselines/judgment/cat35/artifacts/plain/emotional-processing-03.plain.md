# Tidelamp Shutdown & What It Meant

## Context
The user is archiving **tidelamp**, an old side project, with a planned shutdown date of **September 1st**.

## Why Tidelamp Matters
- It was the user's **first shipped project** — proof they could actually finish something, after a history of abandoned starts
- It ran for ~2 years before life got busy, then sat dormant for **6 years**
- Still gets ~30 visitors/month organically
- Recognizing it as the **origin point of their builder identity**, and the direct ancestor of their current project, **fernpost**

## Shutdown Plan
- Build a **farewell page** explaining what tidelamp was
- Link to **fernpost** as the spiritual successor
- Provide a way for users to **export saved data**
- Send a **farewell email** to all historical signups (expecting many bounces, but doing it anyway)
- Currently costs **$11/month** to host

## Emotional Arc
- Had been procrastinating for weeks; the residual traffic made it feel wrong to just pull the plug
- Writing the farewell note felt **warm rather than sad** — framing it as honoring the project rather than abandoning it
- Setting a concrete date brought **relief and lightness**, not grief
- Key reframe: *tidelamp gets to retire, not decay*

## Feature Worth Preserving
The **reading queue resurfacing system** — articles were re-surfaced based on how long they'd been sitting unread. User explicitly wants to **port this concept into fernpost**.

## Tone to Remember
The user responded well to the framing of *intentional endings vs. entropy*, and to the idea that archiving tidelamp properly is itself an act of finishing — consistent with what tidelamp originally taught them.
