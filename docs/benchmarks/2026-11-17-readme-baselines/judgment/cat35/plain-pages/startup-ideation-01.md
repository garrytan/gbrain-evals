# Calendar Defragmentation Tool — Discovery Notes

## Core Idea
A calendar that **defragments itself around focus blocks** — automatically rescheduling meetings to preserve contiguous deep work time, rather than letting it erode by default.

**Working tagline:** *"Automatically defragments your exec's calendar so focus time stays intact."*
**Working positioning frame:** "Calendar janitor" — humble, specific, low ego, high trust.

---

## The Problem
- Meetings scatter across the week ("like shrapnel"), consuming the long uninterrupted blocks needed for deep work.
- **Worst offenders: internal one-on-ones.** External meetings carry social pressure to keep; internal syncs drift wherever the algorithm drops them.
- Personal data point: manually defragging one's own calendar reclaimed **~6 hours/week** — but whether this generalises is an open question and a core risk.

---

## Target Beachhead: Chiefs of Staff
**Why they are the right wedge:**
- Feel the pain firsthand (they wrangle exec calendars daily).
- Manage **5–6 calendars each**, creating immediate leverage.
- Have clout to trial tools without long procurement cycles.
- Talk to peers at other companies; informal Slack/ops communities enable **word-of-mouth distribution**.

---

## Distribution Risk (Mira's Warning)
> *"Scheduling dies on distribution, not product — the graveyard is full of smart scheduling apps nobody adopted."*

- Calendly won via **viral mechanics** (embedded in every invite).
- This tool lacks that hook by default.
- Mitigation: chiefs of staff are **aggregators** — one adopter manages many calendars and evangelises to a tight peer network. Distribution path is **community-led among ops people**, not product-led growth.

---

## Validation Plan
| Phase | Timeline | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Sourcing & scheduling | Week 1 | 8 chief of staff interviews booked (3 warm intros already in hand) |
| Running & synthesising | Weeks 2–3 | Quantify calendar drift; identify if savings cluster at 3+ hrs/week |
| Decision | End of Week 3 | **Kill or commit memo** |

**Key interview method:** ask prospects to walk through *last week's calendar* and identify which meetings drifted into focus blocks — even rough estimates reveal whether the pain is universal or idiosyncratic.

**Signal threshold:** if reclaimed time clusters around **3+ hours/week**, the value prop holds.

---

## Open Risks
1. **Trust** — will people let automation move meetings on their behalf?
2. **Generalisation** — 6 hrs/week reclaimed may be an outlier; 1 hr would collapse the value prop.
3. **API limits** — assessed as manageable for major calendar providers.
