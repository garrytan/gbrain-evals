## Question

On conversations that no system and no gbrain contributor has seen:

1. How does gbrain's 8,000-token memory read compare with three memory servers? (Family A.)
2. How does gbrain's whole-system think loop compare with a file agent working over the same Markdown, with the same
   reader models? (Family B.)
3. How much accuracy does a low-cost model, particle-1, keep in those comparisons, and at what cost per correct answer?

## The set

**Size.** RS1 has 75 conversations in BEAM's 1M tier with 20 probing questions each: 1,500 questions. The cell manifest
counts 80,250,000 cl100k tokens (75 × 1.07 million). Its message count, 152,300, is a placeholder (75 times the public
BEAM-1M mean) until the custodian reports the set's aggregates. A 200-conversation size (O, 4,000 questions, cap
$18,000) is priced and its margins are in verdict.ts, but N is the approved size, and running O is a new decision.

**Generator.** BEAM ([mohammadtavakoli78/BEAM](https://github.com/mohammadtavakoli78/BEAM), commit `b2da22e`), with
its stages plan, question and answer, run by `eval/custody/rs1/mint.sh` with gemini-3.8-flash on a billing-enabled
Google project. Each conversation is truncated at BEAM's 1M-tier limit (1,040,000 tokens, counted as BEAM counts them).
BEAM's Python dependencies install with uv from a hash-pinned lock (`requirements.lock`, `--require-hashes`) into an
isolated Python 3.12 environment, and each paid step runs behind its own metering proxy holding only that provider's
key. Seeds are deterministic per conversation, and a resumed run never re-samples.

**Licenses.** At `b2da22e` (dated 2026-08-31), BEAM's code, the generator included, is MIT (its LICENSE, added
2025-12-12). Its data, the public conversations and probing questions, is CC BY-SA 4.0 (the README's License section).
The public data is read from the GitHub repository at the pinned commit and used under CC BY-SA 4.0 with attribution
([eval/CREDITS.md](../../eval/CREDITS.md)); this repository commits its hashes and aggregates, never its text. RS1 is
written by the MIT code from new private topics, is never shared and publishes aggregates only, so share-alike attaches
to no RS1 file. An earlier draft of this document called BEAM's data MIT, which was wrong.

**Topics.** New topics are generated in custody from a private seed across BEAM's domains, with up to five attempts per
conversation. A topic is rejected when it duplicates one of the 90 public topics of BEAM-100K, 500K and 1M (F28 leaves
out BEAM-10M's 10; see "Custody and exposure") or an accepted RS1 topic: the same normalized title, word overlap (Jaccard) of at least 0.6, or text-embedding-3-large cosine of at least 0.85
(`eval/custody/rs1/rs1_mint.py`).

**Questions.** Two per ability for BEAM's ten abilities (abstention, contradiction resolution, event ordering,
information extraction, instruction following, knowledge update, multi-session reasoning, preference following,
summarization and temporal reasoning), in BEAM's rubric format with graded scoring. Each item cites its supporting turns.

**Validation** (`eval/custody/rs1/validate.ts`). A conversation gate drops the conversation, and its items are not
checked:

- **Topic:** mint.sh dropped it after every topic it drew was too close to a public one.
- **Length:** its cl100k token count is outside the calibrated bounds (next section).
- **Contamination:** its largest share of distinct 13-grams found in any one chat or questions file of the 28 dev-split
  public BEAM conversations at the pinned commit is at or above the calibrated τ (next section; F28 in "Custody and
  exposure"). The share against the union of public files is
  reported, not gated, because independent public BEAM conversations already share 1.2 to 2.1% of their 13-grams with
  the union of the others.
- **Duplicate:** the same chat bytes as an earlier kept conversation, or an estimated 13-gram Jaccard of at least 0.1
  with one.

Item gates apply to both items of each ability. An item fails when its slot is missing; when it duplicates an earlier
item (the same normalized question, or word 3-shingle Jaccard of at least 0.7); when it is contaminated (a public BEAM
question, or half or more of its 13-grams public); when none of the turns it cites is in the truncated chat; or when it
lacks support. Both validators, gemini-3.8-flash and claude-haiku-5-5, must find the reference answer supported by the
cited turns. An abstention item cites nothing, so both validators scan the whole conversation in overlapping windows of
60,000 cl100k tokens with 2,000 tokens of overlap, and the item fails if any window answers it.

**Per-ability floor.** A conversation keeps both items of every ability. A conversation with a failed item is
regenerated up to twice (three selections in all) and is dropped after that. Minting stops when more than 20% of the
items of finished conversations fail.

**Published.** Counts per ability, the token-length distribution, validation pass and drop counts by ability, and the
SHA-256 of the hash list. Nothing else leaves custody.

**Reuse.** RS1 is opened at most three times, each after a committed preregistration. Each opening is logged in the
custodian's access log (custody runs set `GBRAIN_EVALS_CUSTODY_LOG`) and recorded here as a dated amendment, and only
aggregates are published. All 75 conversations are sealed (`eval/decisions/splits/beam-rs1.json`).
`eval/decisions/datasets/rs1-manifest.json` stays unfilled until the custodian records the files' hashes after minting
(PR 3).

### Set gate calibration

The draft's two set gates, a 1% single-file 13-gram share and 1.07 million tokens ± 15%, reject BEAM's own public 1M
conversations: in GBEV-31's stage 0 record, 2 of 3 failed the 13-gram gate and 2 of 5 fell outside the length window.
Calibrated rules replace both. The method was written on 2026-11-03, before any RS1 output was read, and is frozen in
validate.ts (`CALIBRATION`). On 2026-11-04 its subjects and pool were narrowed to BEAM's dev-split conversations (the
allowed set below). The narrowing follows GBEV-3's F28 decision about which BEAM files custody holds, and no RS1 output
informed it.

- **Allowed set (F28, 2026-11-04).** validate.ts reads only the BEAM conversations `eval/decisions/splits` lists as dev.
  Q1's sealed BEAM splits and the 10M tier, which has no dev split, are left out of the public index, the pool and the
  subjects even when a tree holds them, and their files are never opened. F28's sparse custody checkout holds none of
  them.
- **Subjects.** The 11 public BEAM-1M conversations at `b2da22e` that the split lists as dev, each counted on its
  canonical chat file:
  `chat_trunecated.json` when present, else `chat.json`. That is the corpus loader's rule, and the file a minted
  conversation's truncate step produces.
- **Length gate.** low = floor(0.95 × the smallest subject's cl100k count) and high = ceil(1.05 × the largest), by
  validate.ts's own counter. A minted conversation passes when its count lies in [low, high], inclusive. The 5% widening
  covers sizes the public tier happens to lack, and every subject passes by construction.
- **Contamination gate.** For each subject c, s(c) is the largest share of c's distinct 13-grams found in any one public
  source file outside c's own directory (its chat, truncated chat and questions files). The pool is the one the gate
  reads on RS1: every `chat.json` and `probing_questions.json` of an allowed conversation under `chats/`, which at
  `b2da22e` are the dev-split conversations of the 100K, 500K and 1M tiers. τ = 1.25 × the largest s(c), rounded up to
  the next 0.1 percentage point. A minted conversation fails when its single-file share is at least τ. Every subject passes by construction.
- **Related-file guard.** If any subject shares 20% or more of its 13-grams with one other public file, calibration stops
  with `RS1_CALIBRATION_RELATED_FILES`, because files that contain each other would inflate τ. It prints the pair, both
  shares and the pool's sources_sha256, runs no gate and writes no report. A person decides: drop the pair
  (`--calibration-drop`) or keep it (`--calibration-accept`), and the report records the decision. Keeping a pair makes τ
  at least 25%, which the ceiling below always stops, so dropping is the only path to a working gate.
- **Ceiling.** Calibration also stops, with `RS1_CALIBRATION_OUT_OF_RANGE`, when τ is above 5% or is 0, or when the
  bounds reach outside 600,000 to 1,600,000 tokens, because a gate that loose decides nothing.
- **Where it runs.** In custody, inside the validate run that gates RS1, over the allowed subjects and pool. That run sets
  the gate. It is cached by the pool's sources_sha256 and the subjects' paths and sizes, and the validation report
  carries it: the bounds, every subject's share, τ and sources_sha256.
- **Unchanged.** The topic cosine of 0.85, the item 13-gram share of 50%, the item near-duplicate Jaccard of 0.7, the
  conversation near-duplicate Jaccard of 0.1, the 20% stop rule and the per-ability floor.

**Preview on public dev data.** `bun eval/custody/rs1/validate.ts --calibrate-only` ran on the
11 dev-split BEAM-1M conversations, with a tree of the dev-split files of the 100K, 500K and 1M tiers (65 files, no 10M
file, no sealed-split file, each checked against the pinned dataset manifest). The pool held 56 sources (sources_sha256
`c72aa232aafb5f9e6ba1b0533d3fc84bbdf40a59a4ad4caa02b3922d71e1daa5`).

- **Storage.** At rest, RS1 exists only as an age-encrypted archive under the custodian's key. A second encrypted copy

## Readers and judges

**Readers.** claude-opus-5-5 and gpt-6.1-sol read every packed row live, at effort medium, with Q1's output limit of
2,048 tokens. Each reads the same frozen 8,000-token context for a given system and question, and each context is written
once with its SHA-256. particle-1:

- re-reads every packed arm's frozen contexts byte for byte, each checked against its recorded SHA-256
  (`eval/runner/q1/reread-cell.ts`), at effort medium with its 16,000-token output floor. RS1 fixes one effort, medium,
  for every reader, and the receipt records the effort sent and the effort in effect;
- is the file agent's model and think's model, beside Opus and Sol;
- runs only in the crew's paid windows, after the contexts freeze.

Think makes its own model calls: the harness names the reader model, and gbrain sets the rest. claude-opus-5-5 and
gpt-6.1-sol are today's newest frontier Opus and GPT. No older model runs as a reader. gpt-4.1-mini appears only as the
BEAM instrument's canonical judge and inside two systems, and claude-haiku-5-5 only as a judge, a validator and gbrain's
extraction model.

**Counted judges** (`RS1_JUDGES` in `rs1.ts`, `JUDGES` in verdict.ts):

| Reader | Counted judges | An answer's score |
|---|---|---|
| claude-opus-5-5 | gpt-4.1-mini, the BEAM instrument's canonical judge (records under instrument id `beam-rs1`) | that judge's score |
| gpt-6.1-sol | claude-haiku-5-5 (records under `beam-rs1@anthropic:claude-haiku-5-5`) | that judge's score |
| particle-1 | gpt-4.1-mini and claude-haiku-5-5 | the mean of the two scores; the answer counts only when both judges scored it |

particle-1 never judges a counted cell, and no judge grades its own family's rows. `definitionProblems` refuses a plan
that breaks either rule, names an OpenAI-compatible judge or names a judge with no chat route, and the cells' tests check
every RS1 arm. Q1 and F6 predate this rule, and their canonical judge grades GPT rows too.

**Sensitivity judge.** gemini-3.8-flash grades every counted Opus and Sol answer in a 30% conversation sample, chosen by
seed before minting: the 23 of 75 positions with the smallest sha256("rs1-sensitivity", NUL, position). They are
rs1-001, rs1-002, rs1-003, rs1-006, rs1-007, rs1-008, rs1-011, rs1-013, rs1-022, rs1-023, rs1-025, rs1-026, rs1-031,
rs1-036, rs1-038, rs1-040, rs1-046, rs1-057, rs1-060, rs1-061, rs1-069, rs1-072 and rs1-075. It runs in custody at stage
5 (`judge.ts sensitivity`) and prints counts and hashes only. Each family A and B comparison's estimate and interval are
reported under the counted judges and under gemini-3.8-flash. This view is descriptive.

**Disclosed overlaps.** claude-haiku-5-5 is gbrain's fact-extraction model, an item validator and the judge of Sol's
rows. gpt-4.1-mini is the extract-first server's extractor, a model inside the temporal-graph library and the judge of
Opus's rows. gemini-3.8-flash wrote the gold answers, validates items, and is the fallback and sensitivity judge.

### Judge check (end of stage 1, on public BEAM-1M dev answers)

`bun eval/runner/rs1/judge.ts label` writes the label sheet, and `judge.ts check` runs the check. The check reads the
stage 1 cells' own replicate-0 judgments and re-judges nothing the cells judged. It adds gemini-3.8-flash on the keyed
answers and the agreement population only, never on a row of gemini's own family.

1. **Agreement (review d7).** The population is the answers both counted judges are planned to grade: particle-1's rows,
   its re-reads of stage 1's packed contexts included. On it, the share whose pass or fail (score at or above 0.5) agrees
   must be at least 90%, over at least 300 scored pairs. Stage 1 plans 780 particle-1 answers: 40 from think, 40 from the
   file agent and 700 from re-reads (7 packed arms × 100 questions). Without the re-reads there would be 80, under the
   minimum, so the re-reads are part of stage 1.
2. **Beside it, with no bar:** each counted judge's pass/fail agreement with gemini-3.8-flash on that judge's own planned
   rows.
3. **The key (review d8).** Abstention items are keyed by a blind hand label. `label` writes a sheet in a seeded order
   (seed `rs1-judge-check`) holding answer ids and public BEAM text only, with no system, reader, cell or judge names.
   One person labels every stage 1 answer to an abstention item as abstained or answered. hedge.ts at version `hedge-v3`
   is only a pre-filter: it flags candidate abstentions in the design file and the result, and never sets a key. Items of
   five short-answer abilities (information extraction, knowledge update, temporal reasoning, multi-session reasoning
   and contradiction resolution) are keyed per answer when the reference normalizes to a date, a number (with at most two
   unit words) or a name of at most four words. The key reads the answer's final span: correct when the reference
   appears there, not negated, with no other candidate of the same type; wrong when nothing in the whole answer is
   compatible with the reference; otherwise unkeyed, so an ambiguous answer never counts against a judge. The sheet also
   carries a seeded sample of 100 short answers, labeled correct or incorrect, and the key is valid only if it disagrees
   with at most 2% of at least 50 decisive labeled answers.
4. **Keyed accuracy.** Once the key is valid, each counted judge on its own planned rows, and gemini-3.8-flash on every
   keyed answer, must match the key's verdict at pass/fail on at least 95% of at least 100 decisive keyed answers.

The result goes to `judge-check.json` before any exit. If a judge misses a bar, `check` exits with
`RS1_JUDGE_CHECK_FAIL`, and the branch is that gemini-3.8-flash judges every row, recorded here as an amendment before
minting. judge.ts prints that branch as GBEV-3's next step and never switches judges itself.
`RS1_JUDGE_CHECK_KEY_INVALID` means the key failed its validation, not a judge, so the fallback branch does not apply and
the key rules or labels go back to GBEV-3. `RS1_JUDGE_CHECK_TOO_FEW` means a bar's denominator is under its minimum, and
more re-reads or more labels grow it.

**Retirement.** Each shard is judged as soon as it lands. If either counted judge, gpt-4.1-mini or claude-haiku-5-5,
retires mid-campaign, every row is re-judged jointly by the checked fallback judge, gemini-3.8-flash (the judge the stage 1
judge check holds to its keyed-accuracy bar), and the switch is recorded below as a dated amendment with the date the
model retired. The claude-haiku-5-5 case is GBEV-3's decision of 2026-11-04, made before the freeze, and gives that judge
the same rule as gpt-4.1-mini. No runner switches judges by itself. Q1's outcome rules apply: product failures score 0,
harness failures are excluded and reported, and Q1's A24 retired-model rule applies.

## Estimands and statistics

These rules are frozen as code in verdict.ts, and the scoreboard's RS1 mode refuses an `rs1` block whose margins,
interval levels, readers, judges or gate differ from it.

- **Score.** BEAM graded accuracy × 100 per answer. A reader's score for an answer is the mean of its planned judges'
  replicate-0 scores.
- **Missing data.** A product failure scores 0 and stays in. A harness failure, a missing answer, an answer that did not
  fit or a planned judge with no score gives that reader no value. A question enters a comparison only when both rows
  have a value from every reader of the family, and every question left out is counted by reason.
- **Estimand.** Per question, the mean over the family's readers (Opus and Sol) of each reader's score, subject minus
  comparator. The estimate is the question-weighted mean, with conversations as clusters.
- **Family A (component, 8,000 tokens).** gbrain's 8k read (arm P) minus each of the extract-first server, the
  memory-bank server and the temporal-graph library: three comparisons.
- **Family B (whole system).** gbrain think minus the file agent: one comparison.
- **Test.** The restricted wild cluster bootstrap-t (`eval/runner/stats/wild-cluster.ts`), Webb weights, 9,999 draws,
  seed 20261103.
- **Decisive intervals.** Bonferroni: 98.33% (1 - 0.05/3) for each family A comparison and 95% for family B. Holm
  p-values at 0 are secondary. A comparison with no verdict enters Holm at p = 1, so its family keeps its size.
- **Verdicts, in this precedence.** Ahead: the lower bound is above 0. Behind: the upper bound is below 0. Level: the
  interval holds 0 and lies within [-M, +M]. Otherwise inconclusive. M is 3.5 points in family A and 4.1 in family B at
  size N (2.1 and 2.5 at size O).
- **Delivered-context gate.** Each 8k row's delivered context, in cl100k tokens per question, must have a mean and a
  nearest-rank p95 within 7,200 to 8,800, inclusive. A family A comparison whose subject or comparator misses is
  descriptive: an estimate and an interval, no verdict.
- **Comparisons that do not run.** A comparison whose row is not run or invalid, or with fewer than two conversations
  holding a paired question, keeps its place in its family, gets no verdict and blocks the ranking claim.
- **Ranking claim.** "gbrain ranks first among these memory servers" is written only when all three family A comparisons
  are ahead.
- **particle-1 family.** The same four comparisons with particle-1 as the only reader (its packed rows are its
  byte-for-byte re-reads), under the same intervals, margins, gate and rules. It is reported apart and never enters the
  ranking sentence.
- **Descriptive.** The hybrid and floor rows (tabled and gated beside family A, never compared); per-reader and
  per-ability rows; the reader × system interaction (does gbrain's gap grow or shrink under particle-1?); the share of
  gbrain's 8k contexts taken by MCP quote fences, as mean and p95 (GBRA-108's request); and the judge sensitivity above.
- **Null results.** Any null, level or behind verdict for gbrain gets R48's "Why null?" cycle in custody before it is
  reported: exposure, task fit, power and saturation, and the bare model.

## Power

From `power.ts`: the detectable difference at 80% power, two-sided α 0.05 split by Bonferroni across the family, 20
questions per conversation and a between-conversation SD of 2.28 points.

| Family | Variance scenario (source) | SD per conversation, points | N, 75 conversations | O, 200 conversations |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | optimistic (wave 2: 72 public BEAM conversations) | 6.87 | 2.61 | 1.58 |
| A | central (wave 1: 54 sealed BEAM conversations) | 7.72 | 2.94 | 1.78 |
| A | pessimistic (dev pairs: 18 conversations, 360 questions) | 9.14 | 3.48 | 2.11 |
| B | file agent (Q1 family F2's interval, 10 conversations) | 10.6 | 3.47 | 2.11 |

A simulation check with the same bootstrap (1,000 simulations of 999 draws per scenario and size, in
`power-servers.json` and `power-whole.json`) gives Bonferroni power of 0.767 to 0.801 in family A and 0.795 to 0.802 in
family B at those differences. Family-wise error with every true gap at 0 is 0.049 to 0.058 in family A and 0.053 to
0.065 in family B. Holm's power is 0.768 to 0.803. "Level" reaches 80% power at a true gap of 0 for margins of 3.34
points in family A (central scenario) and 4.02 in family B at N, against the fixed M of 3.5 and 4.1. The findings file
reports the realized interval widths.

## Cost per correct answer (first table)

For each reader and row, Opus and particle-1 first, then Sol:

    (ingest dollars per conversation ÷ R + read dollars per question) ÷ graded accuracy

- **R.** 20 reads per stored conversation, with R = 1 and R = 200 beside it.
- **Read dollars.** The system's query calls plus the reader or agent tokens.
- **Ingest dollars.** API calls plus VM time at $0.05 per vCPU-hour.
- **Source.** The receipt's metered cost file, from the metering proxy's usage fields.
