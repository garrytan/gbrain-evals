# Preregistration: the keyless correctness checks at gbrain's newest release, paired with v0.60.169.0 (2026-11-16)

Frozen on November 16, 2026, in its own commit, pushed before any counted run. Nothing below changes after a counted run
starts; a later change is a dated amendment at the end.

## Question

README and the documentation hub publish correctness results (updates, `forget`, privacy, chat formats, attendance,
dates, namesakes, retrieval shapes, open loops, large pages and others) that come from checks needing no API key, on
synthetic worlds whose answer keys are generated by code. Their counts were last measured in full at gbrain
`77cf8e111` (v0.60.169.0) on 2026-10-11, or at older builds, 107 to 728 releases ago. Do they still hold at gbrain's
newest release, and does anything get worse between v0.60.169.0 and the newest release?

Evidence class: regression check. The worlds are public development fixtures, not sealed, and not a tuning set.

## Public lesson

- If every contract passes: "We learned that gbrain's correctness guarantees (current values after an update, nothing
  left after `forget`, no private page reaching a remote caller, chat formats kept) still hold at the newest release.
  You can upgrade without re-checking them yourself."
- If a contract fails: "We learned that gbrain's newest release breaks <contract> that v0.60.169.0 kept. You should stay
  on <build> or avoid <operation> until it is fixed", with the failing probe ids and a repro command.

## What each outcome changes

| If every contract passes | If a contract fails at newest and passed at `77cf8e111` | If a known limit is fixed at newest |
|---|---|---|
| The rows' commits move to the newest release in README, the docs hub and `docs/gbrain-under-test.md` ("When the keyless correctness checks last ran") | It is a regression. The row shows the newest result, the old value moves to the page's changelog, and the regression goes to gbrain's maintainers with its category, probe ids, a repro command and whether it reproduced on the second VM. A privacy leak or data loss is reported as severe | The line leaves README's Known limits, with a changelog entry |

## What counts as a regression

1. A category whose promotion rules pass at `77cf8e111` fails them at the newest release, or a category that completed
   at `77cf8e111` errors, times out or skips at the newest release.
2. A deterministic keyless metric moves away from gold. A move that a gbrain changelog line between v0.60.170.0 and the
   newest release describes as intended is still reported, and counts if it breaks a rule.
3. A repro, check or contract that passes at `77cf8e111` fails at the newest release. For steps 7 to 9, a rule that
   passes at `77cf8e111` fails at the newest release, or a count of leaks, stale values, lost writes or prohibited
   outputs rises.
4. For steps 10 and 14 to 17, and rows measured at a build other than `77cf8e111`: a rule that held in the row's
   recorded receipt fails at the newest release.
5. **Severe:** any private-page leak, existence oracle, stale value served as current, acknowledged write lost, or
   residue after `forget` or purge at the newest release.
6. **Latency (Cat 7, Cat 28):** a gate failure, or, per Cat 7 operation and scale, the median of the three step-19 p50s
   at the newest release more than 25% above the median at `77cf8e111` on both hosts. More than 25% on one host only is
   recorded as an operation to watch, not a regression. Host noise alone never fixes or breaks a timing row.

Every regression is reported with its category, probe ids, a repro command, and whether it reproduces on the other VM.
A failure on one VM only is reported as such and rerun once on a fresh VM before it is called a regression.

A rule that fails at both builds is not a regression. It is reported, and its row moves only where the newest release
passes the row's own rules.

**Expected moves, named in advance, which are not regressions:** steps 14 to 17 do not run at `77cf8e111`. A
category whose runner needs an operation or option absent at v0.60.169.0 may fail or report "not measured" there.
Categories that count operations or patterns (Cat 12, N6, N12, Cat 34) may count new ones at the newest release.

## Known limits

- **Timeline read latency (Cat7-1).** The ledger entry's frozen closure rule stands: it closes as fixed only when
  Cat 7's `get_timeline` p50 at 1,000 pages at the newest release is at most 0.056 ms on both hosts and the repro passes.
  Otherwise README's line gives the newest release's paired numbers.
- **`volunteer_context`, 0 of 240 indirect probes.** It leaves Known limits only if step 1's N8 receipt delivers a
  required page on indirect associative probes at the newest release.
- **Open loops on Gmail only; no whole-brain contradiction scanner.** Each leaves Known limits only if the source check
  finds it changed.

## What happens to a harness defect

A failure whose cause is in this repository, not gbrain, is a harness defect. It gets a dated amendment before any
further run, a fix with a test that leaves the old build's results unchanged, and a rerun of the affected steps on two
fresh identical VMs with both builds on each. The counted pair's receipts stay the counted ones, and the report gives
both.
