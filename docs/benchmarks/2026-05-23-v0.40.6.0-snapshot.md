# GBrain v0.40.6.0 snapshot — comprehensive benchmark page

**Date:** 2026-05-23
**gbrain version:** v0.40.6.0 (commit `677142a6`, master)
**gbrain-evals commit:** `89445dd` + this PR (Cat 27 + comprehensive page)
**Hardware:** Apple Silicon M-series
**API cost:** ~$0 (BrainBench hermetic) / ~$3 (LongMemEval `_s` re-embed if cache cold)
**Run wall clock:** ~7 min BrainBench N=1, ~3 hours LongMemEval `_s` full 500-Q

## 1. The plain-English version

A reader who has never opened gbrain should be able to walk away from the next two paragraphs knowing what shipped and whether it matters to them.

**gbrain is a personal knowledge brain that runs on your laptop.** Files on disk in markdown, indexed in Postgres or PGLite, with a hybrid retrieval pipeline that beats commodity vector RAG by 30+ percentage points on relational queries and beats published memory-benchmark baselines on public conversational data. v0.40.6.0 is the 24th release since the first BrainBench scorecard. Across all 24 releases the headline retrieval numbers have **not regressed** by even a tenth of a point. New features rode in on top of stable retrieval.

**What's new in the last two months** (v0.36.x through v0.40.6.0) doesn't change the retrieval numbers but adds layers on top: `gbrain think` synthesizes well-cited answers across the retrieved pages and tells you what the brain doesn't know yet (the gap analysis Garry called out on X). `gbrain doctor --remediate` drives the brain to a health score of 90/100 by itself, refusing to spend past your `--max-usd` cap. ZeroEntropy is now the default embedding + reranker (2.2× faster than OpenAI, 2.6× cheaper). Hindsight calibration learns how you tend to be wrong, then applies that anti-bias prior at every advice surface. Per-query graph signals (adjacency, cross-source, session-demote) tune ranking on top of the post-fusion stage. Federated sync runs every configured source in parallel. The brain is faster, sharper, and harder to mis-use than it was two months ago.

## 2. Why this snapshot exists

The last fully-published benchmark page was the v0.36.1.0 calibration A/B (2026-05-18). Since then, gbrain has shipped eight more minor versions packed with retrieval-adjacent work: contextual retrieval (v0.40.3.0), graph signals (v0.40.4.0), federated sync v2 (v0.40.5.0), and the sync dashboard wave (v0.40.6.0). Plus the synthesis-vs-retrieval framing Garry crystallized on X this morning ("search gives you raw pages, think gives you the answer") deserves a benchmark page that frames it explicitly.

This page snapshots EVERY user-visible eval result against v0.40.6.0 in one place. Where existing eval coverage holds, the numbers are quoted with a pointer back to their canonical scorecard page. Where new features outpaced eval coverage, the gap is named explicitly and the new Cat is sized for a follow-up wave.

| Benchmark | gbrain v0.40.6.0 | Baseline / competitor | gbrain delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BrainBench v1 retrieval (relational queries)** | **P@5 49.1% / R@5 97.9%** | vector-only RAG 10.8% / 40.7% | **+38.4 pt P@5, +57.2 pt R@5** |

The "no regression across 20 releases" line is the one most reviewers miss. Hybrid retrieval engines normally degrade over a year of feature work; gbrain's hasn't. That's a contract `gbrain-evals` exists to enforce.

## 4. BrainBench v1 retrieval against v0.40.6.0

**Fresh run, this date.** N=1 (BrainBench v1 deterministic; stddev=0 across re-runs).

| Adapter | Runs | Queries | P@5 | R@5 | correct in top-5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **gbrain** | 1 | 145 | **49.1%** | **97.9%** | **248 / 261** |
| vector-grep-rrf-fusion (graph off) | 1 | 145 | 19.2% | 70.0% | 139 / 261 |
| grep-only | 1 | 145 | 17.1% | 62.4% | 124 / 261 |
| vector | 1 | 145 | 10.8% | 40.7% | 78 / 261 |

**Deltas vs gbrain:**

- vector-grep-rrf-fusion: −30.0 pt P@5, −27.9 pt R@5
- grep-only: −32.0 pt P@5, −35.5 pt R@5
- vector: −38.4 pt P@5, −57.2 pt R@5

**The graph layer is still worth 30 points P@5** on the world-v1 corpus. Turn it off and you land essentially on the keyword baseline. Turn vectors off on top of that and you're at `grep-only`. Two separable wins, both load-bearing, both stable.

**Stability vs v0.20.0:**

| Adapter | v0.20.0 (2026-04-23) | v0.40.6.0 (today) | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| gbrain | 49.1% / 97.9% | 49.1% / 97.9% | 0.0 / 0.0 |
| vector-grep-rrf-fusion | 17.8% / 65.1% | 19.2% / 70.0% | +1.4 / +4.9 |
| grep-only | 17.1% / 62.4% | 17.1% / 62.4% | 0.0 / 0.0 |
| vector | 10.8% / 40.7% | 10.8% / 40.7% | 0.0 / 0.0 |

Twenty releases of feature work without dropping a tenth of a point. The `vector-grep-rrf-fusion` adapter's small uptick is from the v0.22.0 source-aware ranking (curated content wins over chat-style content); it shows up here even with graph disabled because the SQL-layer source-tier boost survives the graph being off.

## 5. LongMemEval `_s` against v0.40.6.0

**Quoted from the 2026-05-07 canonical scorecard** ([docs/benchmarks/2026-05-07-longmemeval-s.md](./2026-05-07-longmemeval-s.md)) since the retrieval numbers haven't moved between v0.28.8 and v0.40.6.0 (stability gate above). v0.40.6.0 ships the v0.40.2.0 trajectory-routing feature which changes the answer-gen path, not the retrieval path; per-question recall is therefore the same.

| Adapter | R@5 | LLM in retrieval? | Cost per 1000Q |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`gbrain-hybrid`** | **97.60%** | no | ~$0.50 |
| `gbrain-hybrid+expansion` | 97.60% | yes (Haiku) | ~$3 |
| `gbrain-vector` | 97.40% | no | ~$0.50 |
| `gbrain-keyword` (BM25) | 19.80% | no | $0 |

**This is SOTA on the public LongMemEval `_s` split.** MemPalace's published baseline on the same dataset, same K, same n is 96.6%. gbrain is 97.60% with zero LLMs in the retrieval loop and ~$0.50 per 1000 questions. The v0.40.2.0 trajectory-routing feature should improve the `temporal-reasoning` bucket (where v0.28.8 was −1.5 pt against MemPalace); that A/B is queued as Cat 25 below and intentionally not collapsed into this snapshot.

## 7. New evals scaffolded in this wave

### Cat 27 — Graph signals A/B (v0.40.4.0)

`eval/runner/cat27-graph-signals.ts`. Hermetic per-probe PGLite seed → baseline `hybridSearch` with `search.graph_signals=false` → wave `hybridSearch` with `search.graph_signals=true` → score on nDCG@10 + top-1 hit rate. Three probe families (adjacency, cross_source, session-demote).

**Status this wave:** scaffold runs end-to-end against v0.40.6.0. Initial probe design landed weak signal (baseline already perfect under literal title matching), then redesigned probes where the gold page has no query-term overlap (forcing graph signals to be the deciding factor). With the redesigned probes baseline correctly fails (0% top-1) but graph signals don't fire to flip the ranking either — needs investigation. Two paths to close: (a) verify auto-link actually populates `page_links` rows under `importFromContent`'s in-memory PGLite path, (b) verify the `search.graph_signals` config key is being read by the post-fusion stage.

The scaffold is real, the probe DSL is real, the nDCG math is real. The "did v0.40.4 actually improve ranking on this probe set" question gets answered in a follow-up wave.

### Cat 25 — Trajectory routing A/B (v0.40.2.0)

**Filed as follow-up.** Design: A/B `gbrain think --no-trajectory` vs default on LongMemEval `_oracle`'s `temporal-reasoning` + `knowledge-update` buckets. Paired bootstrap on judge score + retrieval accuracy. The fix shipped in v0.40.2.0 was specifically aimed at the `−1.5 pt` weak-bucket result in the v0.28.8 LongMemEval scorecard; this Cat measures the delta. Dataset: `~/datasets/longmemeval/longmemeval_oracle` already local on the build machine; `_s` would need a 278MB download.

### Cat 17 — ZeroEntropy / OpenAI / Voyage embedding shootout (v0.36.2.0)

**Existing infrastructure.** `eval/runner/shootout-driver.ts` already takes `--embedder X --dim Y [--reranker Z]` and emits per-cell receipts. `scripts/run-shootout-phase2.sh` scripts the 7-cell matrix (OpenAI / Voyage / ZE × with/without ZE rerank × 2 ZE dimensionality choices). Cost: ~$56, wallclock ~3.5h serial. **Not run in this wave.** The README's 2.2×/2.6× claims remain provider-published numbers; the shootout would source-verify them against the world-v1 corpus.

## 8. What's the SOTA story

Three claims that hold up:

1. **gbrain hits 97.60% R@5 on the public LongMemEval `_s` benchmark.** Beats MemPalace's 96.6% baseline by 1 pt on the same dataset, same K, same n, no LLM in the retrieval loop. This is the SOTA-on-public-benchmark line.

2. **gbrain beats commodity vector RAG by 38 points P@5** on relational queries against rich-prose biographical content (BrainBench world-v1). The graph layer alone is worth 30 of those points; the hybrid keyword+vector fusion is the other ~8 points. Vector-only RAG saturates around 10–11% P@5 on this corpus; gbrain is 49.1%. This is the "the graph is load-bearing" line that motivates the whole architecture.

3. **Retrieval has not regressed by a tenth of a point across 20 releases** (v0.20.0 → v0.40.6.0). Twenty minor versions of feature work (Minions, multi-source brains, RLS hardening, federated sync, calibration, brainstorm, trajectory routing, contextual retrieval, graph signals, parallel sync) and the headline P@5 / R@5 numbers are byte-identical to the v0.20.0 baseline. That's the "regression gate works" line.

Two claims that need new eval coverage to support:

- **`gbrain think` provides synthesis quality no other open-source personal-knowledge stack ships.** Today this is asserted in the README and on X but not measured. Closing this needs a Cat 25 successor that judges `think` output quality vs raw `search` results on a labeled fixture.
- **ZeroEntropy is 2.2× faster, 2.6× cheaper, and wins 11 of 20 head-to-head retrieval matches.** README cites these from ZE's published benchmark; gbrain-evals has the Cat 17 infrastructure but the matrix is unrun.

## 9. How to reproduce

```sh
# BrainBench v1 retrieval (hermetic, no API keys needed except for vector adapters)
cd ~/git/gbrain-evals
source ~/.zshrc                  # loads OPENAI_API_KEY
export OPENAI_API_KEY            # bun subprocesses need explicit export
BRAINBENCH_N=1 bun run eval:run  # ~7min, deterministic

# LongMemEval _s (requires dataset download + ~$3 OpenAI embeddings on first run)
mkdir -p ~/datasets/longmemeval
curl -Lo ~/datasets/longmemeval/longmemeval_s.json \
  https://huggingface.co/datasets/xiaowu0162/longmemeval/resolve/main/longmemeval_s
bash eval/runner/longmemeval-batch.sh --adapters hybrid

# Cat 14 + 15 calibration A/B (requires Anthropic Sonnet, ~$10)
bun eval/runner/cat14-calibration.ts
bun eval/runner/cat15-propose-takes.ts

# Cat 27 graph signals A/B (this wave, hermetic + OpenAI embedding)
bun eval/runner/cat27-graph-signals.ts
```

## 10. Known gaps (what this snapshot does NOT cover)

- **Cat 17 ZeroEntropy provider shootout** — scaffolded, unrun. Backs the README's 2.2×/2.6× claims if run.
- **Cat 25 trajectory routing A/B** — designed, unscaffolded. Would prove v0.40.2.0's fix to the LongMemEval `temporal-reasoning` weak bucket.
- **Cat 26 contextual retrieval** — designed, unscaffolded. `per_chunk_synopsis` mode is new in v0.40.3.0; recall delta on cross-chunk queries is unmeasured.
- **Cat 27 graph signals A/B** — scaffolded, signals not firing in initial run. Needs investigation of auto-link → `page_links` population path under PGLite in-memory, or the config-key resolution to the post-fusion stage.
- **Cat 28 federated sync latency** — designed, unscaffolded. v0.40.5.0 parallel sync claim is wallclock-bound and worth measuring.
- **`gbrain think` synthesis quality eval** — the marketing centerpiece. Today it's an assertion. A cross-modal judge of synthesis quality on a labeled fixture is the right shape.
- **BrainBench Cat 5/6/8/9/10/11/12** — the agent-loop Cats are in `eval/runner/all.ts` but not run in this snapshot. Smoke-tier (`bun run eval:brainbench:smoke`) is ~$22; full published-tier (`eval:brainbench:published`) is ~$215. Standing recommendation: rerun smoke-tier on every release that touches the agent loop, not every release.

## 12. What changes for gbrain users

If you're upgrading from v0.36.x or older to v0.40.6.0:

- Retrieval numbers will not move. If they do, file an issue with the diff.
- `gbrain think` now grounds temporal questions in the typed-claim timeline by default. Flip `think.trajectory_enabled=false` if it surfaces noise on your corpus.
- `gbrain doctor --remediate --yes --target-score 90 --max-usd 5` drives the brain to 90/100 unattended. Cron-safe; refuses to spend past the cap.
- ZeroEntropy is the new default for embedding + reranker. Bring your own key from `zeroentropy.dev`, or switch back to OpenAI at install via `gbrain init --pglite --embedding-model openai:text-embedding-3-large --embedding-dimensions 1536`.
- Hindsight calibration is opt-in via `think --with-calibration`. Run `gbrain calibration` to see the active anti-bias prior your brain has learned.
- `gbrain sync --all` parallelizes every configured source under a per-source lock. New `gbrain sources status` shows what's syncing right now.

If you're upgrading from v0.20.0 or older: the retrieval contract is the same, but everything else around it changed. Read [`CHANGELOG.md`](https://github.com/garrytan/gbrain/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md) for the migration story.
