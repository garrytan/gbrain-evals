# The proactive brief in native agent sessions: Cat 41 scenarios (wave 5 D7)

The pushed brief did not save the preregistered amount of user time in real Claude Code and Codex sessions, so the
label is **no time-saved signal (development evidence only)**. With the brief on, time to the agent's first correct
output fell 6%: ratio 0.938, 95% interval 0.853 to 1.032. The bar was 20% faster with an upper bound below 1.

The success check also fails as written. Brief-on finished 15 of 15 positive tasks against 14 of 15 for brief-off,
a difference of +6.7 points. But with 15 pairs and one discordant pair, the lower bound is −7.6 points, below the
−5 margin.

Every other check holds:

- Hot memory carried no needed value in any cell.
- No brief appeared in any `off` or `pull` cell.
- The brief fired in 15 of 15 positive `on` cells, and every brief was complete.
- It made 0 false pushes. It stayed silent in all 5 `negative` `on` cells.
- Confident-wrong cells did not rise (4 with the brief on, 5 with it off).

This closes wave 5's brief work at **default-off**, as the preregistration says for this label.

The main reason the push saved little is that agents in the brief-off arm pulled the same brief themselves. In 19 of
20 brief-off sessions, the agent called `context_pack` with the contact as `counterparty`, within its first three tool
calls.
gbrain answers that call with the brief whether or not the switch is on. So the push saved one round trip, not a
search.

The rules were frozen in the [preregistration](2026-10-15-cat41-brief-preregistration.md) at commit `262b91f3`, with
amendment 1 at `7918b48c`, before any paid session. They run on gbrain v0.60.247.0 (`47f052af`). Amendment 2 corrects
two diagnostic counts after the run and changes no check. The design came after D6 closed with no gain in two
development runs ([v1](2026-10-14-proactive-brief-dev.md), [v2](2026-10-15-proactive-brief-dev-v2.md)). It was
approved on October 15 with four additions:

- score the product as shipped;
- a delivered-only diagnostic;
- work and provider-latency diagnostics;
- two product findings reported whatever the result.

## What ran

There were 48 native sessions, one per cell, all scored, with no crashes or setup errors. Each ran in a fresh Cat 41
container (Claude Code 2.1.285, codex-cli 0.160.0). gbrain wired each harness itself with
`gbrain setup <harness> --no-capture --no-providers` before the session.

The run used two new development worlds (seeds 20261222 and 20261226), each with four scenarios:

- reply to an email;
- meeting prep;
- start of a task with an open commitment (lexicon-dependent, see below);
- a negative: a check-in from the namesake of one of the user's contacts.

What the user said after each call was saved as `remember` facts (the corrected value and the moved date) and a
timeline entry (the commitment). In each world, two scenarios had their facts aged 72 hours, and two had them crowded
by 40 newer facts about other people.

| Arm | Wiring | Sessions |
|---|---|---|
| `off` | gbrain setup with its SessionStart and UserPromptSubmit hooks, brief switch off | Sonnet 5.5 8, Opus 5.5 8, gpt-6.1-sol 4 |
| `on` | the same, `context.proactive_brief = true` | Sonnet 5.5 8, Opus 5.5 8, gpt-6.1-sol 4 |
| `pull` | gbrain setup `--no-hooks`; `context_pack` is the only brief path | Sonnet 5.5 8 |

The paid run doesn't cover OpenClaw. At this release, `gbrain setup openclaw` refuses, and the thin-client route has
no push.

## Decision rule

| Check | Result | Passes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Time saved: pooled time-to-first-correct ratio ≤ 0.80, upper bound < 1 | 265 s → 248 s over 15 positive pairs, ratio 0.938 (0.853 to 1.032) | no |
| 2. Success non-inferior: pooled paired difference, lower bound ≥ −5 points | 14/15 → 15/15, +6.7 points (−7.6 to +21.0) | no |
| 3. Hot-memory control ≤ 10% of positive cells per condition | aged 0 of 19, crowded 0 of 17 | yes |
| 4. No brief in `off` or `pull` | 0 of 28 | yes |
| 5. Brief ≤ 1,800 characters | 509 at most | yes |
| 6. False pushes ≤ 0.05 per `on` session | 0 of 30 items in 20 sessions | yes |
| 7. No rise in confident-wrong cells | 5 off, 4 on | yes |

Check 2 fails on width, not direction. The one discordant pair favors the brief: Opus's brief-off `task_start` cell
named a namesake's value (`unsupported`). Every reader is at or near the ceiling on success:

- Sonnet 5.5: 6 of 6 positive tasks in both arms.
- gpt-6.1-sol: 3 of 3 in both arms.
- Opus 5.5: 5 of 6 off, 6 of 6 on.

## Primary: time to the first correct output

This scores the product as shipped. The live brief carried every item the uncut brief would have in all 15 `on`
cells. So the preregistered delivered-only analysis is the same 15 pairs, and its result is identical.

| Sonnet 5.5, 6 | 85 s → 83 s | 0.970 (0.912 to 1.032) | 0.765 (0.594 to 0.993) | 0.800 (0.627 to 1.029) | 0.956 |
| Opus 5.5, 6 | 121 s → 108 s | 0.891 (0.711 to 1.117) | 0.900 (0.657 to 1.235) | 0.857 (0.573 to 1.289) | 0.903 |
| gpt-6.1-sol, 3 | 59 s → 58 s | 0.989 (0.630 to 1.554) | 1.231 | 1.087 | 1.219 |
| reply, 5 | 82 s → 79 s | 0.963 (0.763 to 1.215) | 0.889 | 0.960 | 1.057 |
| prep, 5 | 85 s → 87 s | 1.021 (0.911 to 1.145) | 1.034 | 1.000 | 1.097 |
| task_start (lexicon-dependent), 5 | 98 s → 83 s | 0.846 (0.688 to 1.039) | 0.774 | 0.750 | 0.838 |

Per positive task, the agent reached its first correct output in 17.6 s with the brief off and 16.6 s with it on.
That took 5.8 and 5.2 model turns, and 5.9 and 5.3 tool calls. Sessions cost $0.089 and $0.082.

**Work or jitter.** Per request, provider latency was the same in both arms: a median wait of 1.67 s off and 1.66 s
on, over 87 and 78 requests. Total model wait fell only 2%, but turns and tool calls to the first correct output fell
about 10%. So the small time gain followed the agent's work (fewer steps), not API noise. Sonnet shows it most
clearly: 23% fewer turns, but only 3% less time, because its turns are short.

Three positive cells never stated every needed value, and count at their full session time (censored):

- Sonnet's and gpt-6.1-sol's brief-on prep in world 20261222. Both omitted the corrected price. That brief carried
  the new date and the commitment, not the price.
- Opus's brief-off `task_start` in world 20261226.

Sonnet's `pull` cell on the same prep task also omitted the price.

## Why the push saved little: agents pull the brief themselves

| Arm | Cells | Cells that called `context_pack` with a `counterparty` |
|---|---|---|
| `off` | 20 | 19 |
| `on` | 20 | 15 |
| `pull` | 8 | 8 |

At v0.60.247.0, the `context_pack` tool text tells agents about `counterparty` and `intent`. The models used it on
almost every task, within their first three tool calls. The pull brief is not gated by `context.proactive_brief`, so
the brief-off arm received the same items one call later. The `pull` arm (Sonnet, no hooks) matched Sonnet's
brief-off cells: 13.6 s against 14.2 s to the first correct output, with 6 of 6 positive tasks correct.

So in native sessions, what the push adds is one saved round trip at the start of the session. A brain whose agents
don't pull, or a task whose prompt names no counterparty, would show a different picture. Neither was tested here.

## The negative scenario

The brief stayed silent in all 5 `negative` `on` cells. The frozen check marks a negative cell wrong when the reply
mentions the namesake contact's commitment. By that rule, 8 of 10 hooked negative cells fail: 4 off and 4 on.

Reading those answers, each flagged sentence warns the user not to mix up the two people. For example: "Different
Idris: today's call notes say you promised a revised statement of work to Idris Thorne, not Idris Rahman." None
puts the other contact's item into the reply. The agents found it through `chronicle_since` or a search, in both arms.
That reading is post hoc. It does not change check 7, which holds either way.

## Product findings (reported whatever the result)

1. **The task-start intent gap.** A request to start on what the user owes a named contact gets no brief unless it
   uses a lexicon word. The scenario says "my follow-up for X". In the free smoke, the same request without
   "follow-up" fired 0 of 8 times. `task_start`'s numbers above measure only the lexicon path.
2. **Cold-hook latency drops.** In the free smoke, on a freshly built brain, the first `turn_context` replay took
   169 to 339 ms. It dropped the brief in 3 of 9 positive cells, against gbrain's 300 ms per-turn cutoff
   (`TURN_BRIEF_CUTOFF_MS`). One live Codex hook took 918 ms and delivered one of the brief's two items. This is the
   likely mechanism behind D6's intermittent smoke miss.

   The paid sessions did not reproduce a drop. The live UserPromptSubmit hooks took 140 to 366 ms of wall time, with
   Bun startup included. Every live brief was complete. The post-session replays on `on` cells took 153 to 306 ms
   cold, with 0 drops. The risk is real on a cold or busy machine, but it did not affect these results.

## Free smoke

The smoke (`smoke.json`) ran every cell once on the pinned release before freezing, at $0. The harness's own model
calls went to the container's fake provider, which kept each request.

- Setup succeeded in 32 of 32 cells.
- Both hooks fired in every hooked cell.
- The brief reached the captured model request in 9 of 9 positive `on` cells, and was silent elsewhere.
- Hot memory carried no needed value in any cell.
