# RS1 stopped at stage 0: one minted conversation costs $28.85 to $40.63, and $2,000 can't resolve the gap RS1 was built for

Measured November 3 to 4, 2026. The mint ran BEAM's own generation pipeline (BEAM `b2da22e`, generator
`gemini-3.8-flash`) through gbrain-evals' custody scripts at main `2c54733f`, and validation ran at `153528e3`. Run in
thread GBEV-36 (issue GST-1154) on budget-ledger run `rs1-stage0-2026-11-03T13-59-09-657Z-39d1056b`. Every figure here
is a count or a cost. This page holds no RS1 text, every conversation stage 0 made came from a public BEAM topic, and no
sealed conversation was read or produced.

## The finding

RS1, the ranking set, was a plan for a new sealed benchmark big enough to rank gbrain among memory servers. No sealed set
we hold can do that today. F6's BEAM-10M comparison detects only differences of about 13.6 points. On F6's BEAM-1M
sealed split, gbrain's 8,000-token read (gbrain `0ccf2bdd`) leads the strongest external system, an extract-first
memory server, by 4.8 points (95% interval 2.6 to 7.1), but its 24 conversations describe the systems and can't rank
them ([F6 scoreboard](2026-10-29-f6-current-master/scoreboard.md)). RS1 would have minted 75 new million-token
conversations in BEAM's format, kept them in custody, and compared gbrain with three memory servers and gbrain's
`think` loop with a file agent.

Stage 0 measured what one conversation costs. One conversation minted on fixed code (c003) cost **$40.63** to mint and
validate, about twice the $20 the plan assumed. BEAM's own truncation throws away the last three of its ten answer
batches, and stopping generation at the truncation point cuts the cost to **$28.85**. A replay on c003 shows the early
stop leaves every file BEAM keeps byte-identical. c003 passes every conversation gate, and 15 of its 20 items pass
validation.

At those prices the full design costs about $8,200 all-in. A design that fits the $2,000 limit on each eval detects only
gaps of about 6.5 to 7.7 points, so it can't resolve the 4.8-point gap RS1 was built for. Garry Tan stopped RS1 at stage
0 on November 4, 2026. He plans to revisit it once judge robustness (GST-1365) is settled.

## Why RS1 stopped

Garry approved RS1 on November 3 at 75 conversations under a $7,000 cap. Later that day he capped every eval at $2,000
all-in from 13:30 UTC, counting paid calls, judges, VMs and the agent threads' own model use: "Pare down the evals to $2K
per skill".

On November 4 he got three options:

- **R1 (recommended):** stop RS1 at stage 0, write up its findings, and revisit after GST-1365.
- **R2:** an exception of up to $11,000 all-in for the full design.
- **R3:** the $2,000 version, at about 13 conversations.

He answered "do it", which takes the recommendation, R1. GST-1365 asks whether `gpt-4.1-mini`, the canonical judge of
the Q1 and F6 scoreboards, ranks systems the same way a frontier judge does. RS1's draft preregistration uses
`gpt-4.1-mini` as a counted judge, so that answer bears on RS1's design.

## What a mint does

A mint is one run of BEAM's pipeline for one conversation. It picks a topic, writes a plan, writes the user's questions
in 10 batches (63 each on c003), then generates the conversation around them batch by batch, so the chat grows past a
million tokens.
BEAM then truncates the chat to its length limit, writes probing questions about what the chat contains, and selects two
items for each of ten abilities (such as `temporal_reasoning` or `abstention`), 20 in all. Our `validate.ts` then
checks the conversation (topic, length, contamination by public BEAM text, duplicates) and each item. Among other checks,
an item passes only when two validator models, `gemini-3.8-flash` and `claude-haiku-5-5`, each find its reference answer supported by the turns
it cites. An abstention item, whose right answer is "the chat doesn't say", passes only when neither validator finds an
answer anywhere in the chat.

Stage 0 minted three conversations from public BEAM-1M topics:

- **c001 and c002** ran on code from before the reasoning-budget fix (#362).
- **c003** ran on main `2c54733f` with every fix below, through all ten answer batches, and was validated at `153528e3`.

estimate: stage 0 used public topics, and a sealed mint's topic step (one call per attempt plus an embedding check)
hasn't been measured. The early stop saves the $11.78 that batches 8 to 10 cost on c003.

### BEAM's truncation throws away three of ten answer batches

The mint's `truncate` step calls BEAM's `adjust_chats_length.count_chats_tokens` with a limit of 1,040,000 tokens. It
keeps turns in order until a running `gpt-4o-mini` (o200k) token count passes the limit, drops every later turn, and
truncates the plan to match. Probing questions, selection and the rubric all read the truncated chat.

On c003 the full chat holds 1,550 messages and 1,575,450 cl100k tokens. BEAM cut inside batch 7, keeping 26 of its 63
turns: 7 of 10 batches, 404 of 630 turns and 1,018 of 1,550 messages. BEAM's count at the cut was 1,042,414. The kept
chat is 1,034,957 cl100k tokens, inside validation's window of 909,500 to 1,230,500. **Batches 8 to 10 cost $11.78 over
1,077 requests, and nothing from them survives.**

Stopping early changes nothing BEAM keeps. BEAM's `main.answer_generation` builds batch b from plans 0 to b only (its
`previous_plans` loop), so no later batch feeds a kept turn. Draft PR #367 adds the early stop: after each batch it runs
BEAM's own `convert_chats_pickle_to_json`, `add_ids_to_chats` and `count_chats_tokens` on a scratch copy, and stops
after the first batch in which BEAM's truncation would cut. It reads the cut from the `chat_trunecated.json` file BEAM writes
(BEAM's spelling), never from BEAM's printed numbers. Its `check-early-stop` command replays the stop on a finished mint
and compares BEAM's four truncated files from the full chat and from the stopped prefix, byte for byte.

On c003, at #367's head `6c026efc`, the replay stopped after batch 7. All four files matched
(`chat_trunecated.json`, `chat_trunecated.pickle`, `plan_new_trunecated.pickle` and `plan_new_trunecated.txt`),
with `files_differ=0`, and the test suite backed by a real BEAM checkout passed 9 of 9. This is one conversation. CI
skips the real-BEAM cases, because CI has no BEAM checkout, and #367 is not merged, so main's mint still pays for the
discarded batches.

### The reasoning budget left the question step short

BEAM sizes each call's `max_tokens` for a model that doesn't reason. `gemini-3.8-flash` reasons before it answers,
and at its default level its reasoning tokens counted against the same output limit. On c001 and c002 the question step
spent 833,706 and 883,087 reasoning tokens over 319 and 320 calls, and every one of the ten batches came back short: the
shortest held 36 and 34 lines, against a floor of 58. The chats came out at 859,570 and 715,901 cl100k tokens, so both failed the length
gate and no item was checked.

#362 sends thinking level `low`, the lowest the model accepts, and asks for BEAM's `max_tokens` plus 8,192 tokens. On
c003 the question step's 110 calls used 0 reasoning tokens each, and every batch held 63 lines, as BEAM's published
coding batches do (60 to 63). The step cost $2.02, against $4.86 and $4.53 before.

### c003's validation

c003 passes every conversation gate: topic, length (1,034,957 tokens), contamination (the largest share of its 13-grams
found in one public BEAM file is 0.000080, against a 1% bar; the share in the union of public files, reported only, is
0.000108) and duplication.

Of its 20 items, 15 pass and 5 fail, so its verdict is `regenerate`: a conversation keeps both items of every ability,
and a failed item is regenerated up to twice before the conversation is dropped. No validator call errored.

| Ability | Items passed |
|---|---|
| `temporal_reasoning` | 0 of 2 |
| `abstention`, `contradiction_resolution`, `information_extraction` | 1 of 2 each |
| `event_ordering`, `instruction_following`, `knowledge_update`, `multi_session_reasoning`, `preference_following`, `summarization` | 2 of 2 each |

The support gate failed four items for Haiku and three for Gemini. The absence gate failed one abstention item for each
validator. Both validators failed the same items except one `temporal_reasoning` item, which only Haiku failed.

A repeat run with its own cache gave identical verdicts. Haiku, which now samples at the provider's default setting
(#366), matched itself on 18 of 18 support verdicts and 38 of 38 absence verdicts, and Gemini, the control, matched on 18
of 18 and 38 of 38. The two runs cost $1.49 and $0.66.

**One conversation is a small sample.** Its 75% item pass rate has an exact 95% interval of about 51% to 91%, and one
conversation can't say whether `temporal_reasoning`'s two failures come from the generator, the validators or chance.

c001 and c002 gave no item rates. `claude-haiku-5-5` rejected `temperature: 0`, so all 55 of its calls failed (fixed
by #366). Both chats also predate the message-id renumbering, so all 33 and all 44 cited ids repeat in their chats, and
validation would have handed the validators the wrong turns.

## What RS1 would cost

The plan's money check, gate A, passes when the projected paid cost is at most the cap divided by 1.1: $6,363.64 at the
$7,000 cap. The minimum detectable difference (MDD) below is the gap the design would detect with 80% power.

### Keeping the design: 75 conversations

At 75 conversations the MDD is 2.94 points (central) to 3.48 (pessimistic) for family A, gbrain's 8,000-token read
against each of the three memory servers, and 3.47 points for family B, gbrain's `think` loop against the file agent.
These rows come from `pricing.ts` at #364's head, with stage 0 at its actual $110.95, the one measured conversation
passing (1 of 1) and a 10% reserve. "All-in" adds about $780 of agent-thread credits already spent and about $700
projected for the rest of the program (range $400 to $900).

| Mint cost | Item regeneration | `think` row | Paid | Gate A ($6,363.64) | Cap gate A needs | All-in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $40.63, as minted | 25% | yes | $8,363 | fails | $9,200 | about $9,840 |
| $28.85, early stop | 25% | yes | $7,149 | fails | $7,900 | about $8,630 |
| $28.85, early stop | 0% | yes | $6,554 | fails | $7,250 | about $8,030 |
| $28.85, early stop | 0% | no | $5,776 | passes | $6,400 | about $7,260 |
| $40.63, as minted | 0% | yes | $7,525 | fails | $8,300 | about $9,000 |

`pricing.ts` charges each item regeneration as a full re-mint, so the 25% rows overstate it. Regeneration re-runs only
the probing-question steps, about $2.32 on c003. With the early stop and that cost (about $190 at 75 conversations), the
full design comes to about $6,750 paid and **$8,200 all-in**. Of the rows above, only the one without the `think` row,
and so without family B, passes gate A at $7,000. The earlier two-batch projection ($32.10 with the early stop) found
that 69 conversations would also pass, raising the MDD by 0.13 points in family A and 0.16 in family B.

### The $2,000 version

About $890 was already spent. Keeping $250 to $500 for agent-thread work leaves about $760 for paid calls. The best
design that fits compares gbrain's shipped defaults with the strongest external server from F6 only: one comparison,
three answer models, the early stop, no `think` or file-agent rows, a 5-conversation dry run on public data at about $65
and a 10% reserve.

At about $46.40 a conversation that buys about **13 conversations** (10 to 15), with an MDD of **6.5 points central and
7.7 pessimistic** (6.0 to 9.1 across that range). Its power to detect F6's 4.8-point gap is about 52% central and 39%
pessimistic. Detecting 4.8 points with 80% power needs 23 to 31 conversations, about $2,480 to $2,890 all-in. Keeping
all three servers at 9 conversations gives an MDD of 10.1 to 11.9 points, and family B alone gives 11.3. Garry declined
this version.

## What to use and what to avoid

For any mint of BEAM-format conversations with BEAM's pipeline:

- **Stop at the truncation point.** Three of ten answer batches are paid for and thrown away otherwise, about $11.78 a
  conversation on c003. #367 has the stop and its byte-for-byte check.
- **Give a reasoning generator its own budget.** Send `gemini-3.8-flash` thinking level `low` and room above BEAM's
  `max_tokens` (#362), or the question step comes back short and the chat fails the length gate.
- **Price from one finished conversation.** The two-batch projection ($42.78) was close to the finished figure
  ($40.63), but only the finished conversation showed the real cut point and the item pass rate.
- **Run long paid mints on a rented VM** with a single ledger writer and a resume path, not on a development machine
  that can pause.
