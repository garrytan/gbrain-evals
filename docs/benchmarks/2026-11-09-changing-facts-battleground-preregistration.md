## The question

On sealed, made-up chat histories in which facts change in known ways, does gbrain at its stable release, reading 8,000
tokens of memory, answer questions about what is true now more accurately than each of the memory-bank server, the
extract-first server and the temporal-graph library?

This is a superiority question, asked where gbrain's design gives it a reason to win. A loss is reported the same way
as a win.

## Public lesson

- **If gbrain wins:** "We learned that keeping old facts with end dates, and serving current facts first, answers
  questions after corrections and repeated stale values better than overwriting them or plain search. You should store
  a change as a fact that supersedes the old one, not as an edit."
- **If it loses or shows no measurable difference:** "We learned that the <winning system>'s approach handles changing
  facts as well as or better than gbrain on <these kinds of change>. You should <what that design does>." The losing
  kinds of change go to gbrain's maintainers as issues, each with where the answer was lost (retrieval or reading).

## Why gbrain should win, and why the answer is not known

gbrain stores the original notes as Markdown and extracts facts from them. When a fact changes, gbrain closes the old
fact with an end date (`valid_until`) and marks what replaced it, instead of deleting it, and its search serves facts
that are still valid, ahead of pages, in a facts block. So a question like "where does Ana live now?" after Ana has
moved, after a wrong statement was corrected, or after the old city was mentioned many times and the new one only
once, should reach the reader with the current value first and dated.

Our own record on this is mixed, which is why the test is worth running:

- **The update-and-forget test of 2026-10-06** (report-only, five seeds): gbrain chose the current value 29 of 60 times
  and the value at a past date 17 of 60 times. The servers scored 45 to 58 and 24 to 55. gbrain's items reached the
  reader with no dates then ([shootout report](2026-10-06-oss-memory-shootout.md), "Update and forget").
- **Memory-proof wave 1** (sealed BEAM, one comparator, an older build): gbrain was ahead on contradiction resolution
  (+8.8 points) and knowledge update (+3.7), and behind on temporal reasoning (-6.9) and event ordering (-7.9)
  ([results](2026-10-09-memory-proof-wave-sealed-results.md)).
- **Cat 40 Hard, world M** (agents with gbrain against agents with files): gbrain finished 47 of 60 conflicting-source
  tasks against 56, -15 points (95% interval -23.3 to -6.7). On the sealed world S it finished 52 against 49
  ([confirmation](2026-10-10-model-ladder-hard-confirmation.md)). This is gbrain's one losing task family so far.

## The set

**Worlds.** Each world is a made-up chat history of about 200,000 cl100k tokens: 140 to 160 dated sessions between a
user and an assistant over about 18 months. It tracks 30 facts ("slots") about fictional people, companies and
projects, with near-name decoy entities and ordinary filler chat. Each world asks 64 questions, eight in each of eight
kinds. Each question is asked in one of two forms, exact or paraphrased, balanced by seed.

| Kind | Family | What the history does | Example question (invented) |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 updated | primary | A value changes once or in a chain (A, then B, then C). Some chains also announce a change that has not taken effect by the end of the history | "Which city is Ana Okafor based in now?" |
| S2 corrected | primary | A value is stated, and later the user says it was wrong all along | "What's the renewal price on the Harbor deal?" |
| S3 repeated stale | primary | The old value comes up in 8 to 15 sessions, and the new one is said once, later | "Who runs operations at Fenwick now?" |
| S4 conflicting sources | primary | 3 to 5 sources disagree in the same period: a signed or owner-confirmed statement in the middle of a long session, and later drafts, recaps or agent notes with other values | "What price did we actually sign with Harbor?" |
| S5 withdrawn | primary | A fact is retracted, so nothing holds now | "Which vendor handles Fenwick's payroll now?" |
| S6 as-of | secondary | A value over time, asked at a past date | "Where was Ana based in June 2026?" |
| S7 as-known | secondary | A change learned late, or a correction, asked as what the user had been told by a past date | "On 3 May, which city had I been told Ana lived in?" |
| S8 history | secondary | The values in order | "List Ana's cities, oldest first." |

**Gold never comes from a model.** A seeded plan fixes every slot, value, valid-time interval and the session in which
the user learns each value. Each kind's gold comes from an oracle over that plan (`eval/generators/changing-facts-gen.ts`).
gemini-3.8-flash then writes each session's chat around the lines the plan puts in it. Gemini is in no reader's or
judge's model family.

**Dates and "now".** Worlds store dates as day offsets from a world end, and the loader resolves them from
`GBRAIN_EVALS_BG1_WORLD_END`. A dated amendment sets the world end to the day before the counted run. Every question is
asked the day after the world end, so "now" means the same thing to a system that reads the wall clock (gbrain's search
compares `valid_until` with the database's `now()`) and to one that reads the query time. Shifts are whole weeks, so
weekdays stay right. A change the history announces for later is placed after the world end plus the 14-day slip plus
the planned run span, and the runner refuses once the wall clock passes the world end plus 14 days.

**Validation.** Every rendered session is checked before a world is kept:

1. Deterministically, with an alias and implication lexicon for every tracked value: each planned value appears where
   the plan puts it, no tracked or decoy value appears anywhere the plan does not put it, and every date token is intact.
2. Gemini checks the session for extra claims about any tracked slot.
3. A second validator from another family, claude-haiku-5-5, reads every session that touches a tracked slot.

A session that fails is rendered again, up to three times. A world with a session that still fails is dropped. Two
drops among the first five mint slots pause minting (exit 3) and go to GBEV-3.

**Dev and sealed worlds.** Three public dev worlds (`bg1-dev-01` to `bg1-dev-03`) come from public seeds and public
phrasing, and their plans and gold are committed. Stage 1 renders them after this file freezes, and they are committed
with a dated amendment. The sealed set has 48 mint slots (`bg1-001` to `bg1-048`) from a private seed and private
phrasing, minted in slot order. The kept set is the first N_final worlds that pass validation, and minting stops there.

## Readers and judges

**Readers.** claude-opus-5-5 and gpt-6.1-sol read every row live at effort medium with a 2,048-token output limit. Each
reads the same frozen 8,000-token context for a given system and question, written once with its SHA-256. particle-1
re-reads every frozen context byte for byte (`eval/runner/q1/reread-cell.ts`) at effort medium with its 16,000-token
output floor, in crew paid windows. No reader is Sonnet, Haiku, Fable or gpt-5.4-mini.

**Judges (R78).** The instrument asks one short-answer question: given the question, the gold answer and its accepted
forms, is the response correct? It scores 1 or 0. An S8 answer is correct only when the whole list is in the right order.

| Reader | Gating judge | Descriptive view |
|---|---|---|
| claude-opus-5-5 | gpt-6-astra | gemini-3.8-flash |
| gpt-6.1-sol | claude-opus-5-5 | gemini-3.8-flash |
| particle-1 | claude-opus-5-5 (recorded on every row) | gemini-3.8-flash |

- No judge grades its own family's answers, and particle-1 never judges. The cell definitions refuse a plan that
  breaks either rule.
- The gating judges run at the pinned per-reader settings and are cached. The judge model and version are recorded with
  every result.
- The deterministic matcher (`match.ts`) scores every answer as correct, stale, decoy, other or unclear. Its agreement
  with the gating judges is reported, and it never sets a verdict.

**The judge check, before any number counts.** It runs on stage 1's dev answers and on construction items built from
the dev worlds' oracle gold. A paraphrase of the gold is labelled good. A stale value, a decoy entity's value, a
withdrawn fact stated as current, or a wrong date is labelled bad.

- **Gate (i):** Cohen's kappa between Astra and Opus on every item both graded, at least 0.6.
- **Gate (ii):** each gating judge's kappa against the construction labels, which neither judge made, at least 0.6.
- **Size:** the set of items Astra and Opus grade the same way holds at least 40, with at least 15 of each class.
- **Reported beside every result:** each judge's catch rate on bad outputs, false-fail rate on good ones and kappa.
- A failed gate stops the run before any sealed world is minted, and every number goes to GBEV-3.

## Estimand and verdict rules

The rules are written once, in `eval/runner/bg1/rule.ts`. `power.ts` and `verdict.ts` both call it, and a test gives
both the same synthetic data and checks that they reach the same verdicts.

### Primary: superiority over each comparator

1. **Question score.** For each question, a row's score is the mean over the three readers (Opus and Sol live,
   particle-1 from its re-read, joined by question id) of that reader's 0 or 1 under its gating judge.
2. **World difference.** A world's difference is the mean over its primary questions (S1 to S5, 40 per world) of
   gbrain minus the comparator.
3. **Statistic.** The mean of the world differences, with a one-sided paired t test on n - 1 degrees of freedom, where
   n is the number of kept worlds.
4. **Three comparisons:** gbrain minus the memory-bank server, the extract-first server and the temporal-graph library.
   Holm's step-down procedure runs across the three at a family-wise alpha of 0.05.
5. **Verdict words.** "Ahead": the Holm-adjusted one-sided p value for gbrain above the comparator is at most 0.05.
   "Behind": the mirror test, the Holm-adjusted one-sided p value for the comparator above gbrain, is at most 0.05, so a
   loss is called at the same bar as a win. Anything else reads "no measurable difference". "Ahead by more than 5
   points" is reported when the simultaneous Bonferroni lower bound (one-sided, alpha 0.05 / 3) is above 5. It is not
   required for "ahead".

**Why this sizing.** The design is sized so the minimum detectable difference (MDD) at the strictest Holm step
(one-sided alpha 0.0167, 80% power) is at most 5 points. Five points is above the judge shifts GST-1365 measured on
F6's comparisons (-2.7 to +3.1 points), and inside the per-kind gaps memory-proof wave 1 saw on BEAM (+3.7 to +8.8).

### Missing data

- **One common question set.** A question enters only when every system row has a judged value from all three readers.
- **Product failures score 0 and stay in** (`PRODUCT_FAILURES` in `eval/runner/memory-qa/outcomes.ts`: retrieval errors,
  unsupported calls, turn caps, context overflows).
- **Harness failures drop the question.** These are reader, judge or harness errors, budget stops, and missing rows,
  judgments or re-read answers. Every drop is counted by reason and by side.
- **Drop cap.** If more than 5% of a comparison's planned questions drop, that comparison gives no verdict.
- **Worst-case sensitivity.** Every gbrain-side drop scores 0 and every comparator-side drop scores 1, and the three
  tests run again. When the result disagrees with the verdict, the findings' first lines say so.

### Delivered-context gate

A system row passes when its mean fill rate (delivered cl100k tokens over the 8,000-token budget) is at least 0.90. A
failed retrieval counts as 0. A row that fails the gate makes every comparison it enters descriptive.

### Receipts

`verdict.ts` refuses a receipt whose dataset revision is unfilled, whose files' SHA-256 differ from the filled BG1
manifest, or whose kept list is not exactly the first N_final kept worlds.

### Secondary families

Each family has its own Holm correction and uses the same three verdict words. None changes the primary verdict.

1. **Time-indexed (S6 to S8, 24 questions per world):** gbrain minus each comparator.
2. **Design control:** gbrain minus hybrid search on the primary questions. If hybrid search is level with or ahead of
   gbrain, the findings say a win over the comparators "cannot be attributed to gbrain's design", first.
3. **Cost per correct answer:** gbrain's over each comparator's, with "below 1" as the claim and a cluster bootstrap
   over worlds. The first table shows claude-opus-5-5 and particle-1, then gpt-6.1-sol (AGENTS.md), from the metering
   proxy's usage fields. For each reader and row it is (the row's ingest, retrieval and background spend divided by its
   questions, plus the reader's own spend per answer) divided by that reader's accuracy. Judge spend is left out,
   because it buys the measurement, not the answer.

### Reported beside the verdict, not part of it

- Every comparison paired, with its two-sided 95% interval and the number of questions where gbrain did worse (R78). An
  interval that includes zero reads "no measurable difference".
- The restricted wild cluster bootstrap-t (`eval/runner/stats/wild-cluster.ts`, Webb weights, 9,999 draws) as a
  sensitivity check.
- Per-reader and per-kind rows, each labelled unadjusted.
- **Exposure.** For every frozen context: whether the gold value and the stale value were delivered, after alias and
  number normalisation. For the raw-text rows (gbrain's pages and hybrid search) only, whether the deciding line was
  delivered, because extraction rows rewrite the line even when they captured the fact.
- The descriptive Gemini judge view, and the deterministic matcher's agreement with the gating judges.
- Each row's fill rate, the floor (and whether it sits below every system row), the whole-history reference and
  dropped questions by reason and side.

## The loss we already have: Cat 40 H4

H4 asked for one account's authoritative value when an executed contract, a draft amendment, an email summary and an
agent note disagreed, often with the deciding line in the middle of a long document. Our reading, which this test
checks rather than assumes:

- the file agent read whole documents, while ranked chunks favour short, recent, lexically close summaries over the
  governing line;
- authority is not recency, so superseding facts by date can pick the wrong source.

BG1 handles it in three ways:

1. **S4 copies H4's shape into the primary family**, where we predict gbrain does not win.
2. **Exposure checks locate a loss.** They record whether the gold and stale values, and for raw-text rows the
   deciding line, reached the reader. A loss can then be placed in retrieval or in reading.
3. **The whole-history reference stands in for reading everything.**

BG1 does not rerun H4's own comparison, agents with gbrain against agents with files. That stays with Cat 40.

## Predictions, recorded before any data

These are descriptive and are not part of any verdict.

- gbrain ahead on S2 and S3.
- Level on S1 against the memory-bank and extract-first servers, which both scored 58 of 60 on simple updates in the
  update-and-forget test.
- Level or behind on S4, S6 and S8 (Cat 40 H4, and wave 1's temporal and ordering losses).
- Ahead on S7 if the dates of late-learned changes reach the reader.
- The temporal-graph library strong on S6 and S7 by design, and weaker on retrieval overall.

## What would show gbrain does not win

- A comparison that is not "ahead": gbrain does not beat that system here.
- A comparison that is "behind": gbrain loses to it here, reported with the same prominence as a win.
- Hybrid search level with or ahead of gbrain: a win cannot be credited to gbrain's design.
- A kind whose two-sided interval is entirely below 0: a named failure class. When the exposure checks say where the
  answer was lost, it goes to gbrain's improvement lane (GBRA-395) as an issue.

A null or a loss gets R48's "Why null?" cycle in custody before it is reported: exposure, task fit, power and ceiling,
and the whole-history reference.

## Stage 1

Stage 1 runs on public data only, in one GBEV-3 paid window, after this file freezes:

1. **Rendering.** The three dev worlds are rendered and validated. The rendered worlds, with their measured rendering
   cost per world and yield, are committed in a dated amendment.
2. **Every row.** All six rows and the whole-history reference run on the dev worlds, with Opus and Sol live,
   particle-1 re-reads and every judge view.
3. **The judge check** runs as above.
4. **Gold audit.** A dev question on which both Opus and Sol, reading the whole history, disagree with the gold is
   inspected, and any defect is fixed in the generator, in a dated amendment, before the sealed mint.
5. **Pricing.** `pricing.ts` reprices every line from stage 1's measured rates.

Stage 1 stops, and GBEV-3 decides, when:

- a row cannot run;
- a row fails the fill gate;
- items reach the reader undated (the update-and-forget defect);
- the judge check fails;
- the projection passes the plan by 25% (`pricing.ts`'s check-in exit);
- gbrain trails any comparator by more than 5 points on the dev primary. GBEV-3 then chooses between confirming on
  sealed worlds, or handing the failing kinds to GBRA-395 first and running on the fixed pin with the tuning disclosed.

**Raise the ceiling once (R61).** If any comparator scores 95% or more on the dev primary, the generator raises the
ceiling once in a dated amendment (longer histories, more repeats of stale values, more conflicting sources), and new
dev worlds check it.

Dev results are published either way, as development evidence.

## Internal pilot

1. **Wave A** is the first 12 kept worlds through every row, reader and judge.
2. **What leaves custody.** `verdict.ts` in pilot mode emits only the SDs of the three per-world differences and their
   correlations, never means. A test checks that its output has no mean keys.
3. **Sizing.** `power.ts` sets N_required, the smallest N with simulated power of at least 80% for every comparison at
   a true difference of 5 points under Holm, and N_final = min(40, max(30, N_required)), with N_required the largest
   over the three comparisons.
4. **Over the cap.** If N_required is above 40, the run pauses (exit 3) with the MDD reachable at 40, and GBEV-3 decides.

Resizing from the pilot's own SDs is Wittes and Brittain's internal pilot. Its small rise in type I error above the
nominal level is accepted. The means stay in custody, so the resize cannot react to how gbrain is doing. Wave B's cells
are regenerated by a dated amendment once N_final is known.

## Power

The per-world SD of a difference is modelled as sqrt(tau^2 + sigma^2 / k), where k is the number of questions in the
family, sigma the per-question SD of a paired difference of binary scores, and tau the between-world SD of the true gap.
The scenarios come from F6's and wave 1's paired BEAM variance, rescaled to binary scoring: optimistic (sigma 43,
tau 2), central (47, 3) and pessimistic (54, 4). The arithmetic is reproduced by `power.ts`.

| Family (questions per world) | Scenario | Per-world SD | MDD at N = 30 | MDD at N = 40 | N for an MDD of 5 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Primary (40) | optimistic | 7.09 | 4.00 | 3.43 | 21 |
| Primary (40) | **central** | **8.01** | **4.52** | **3.87** | **25** |
| Primary (40) | pessimistic | 9.43 | 5.32 | 4.56 | 34 |
| Time-indexed (24) | optimistic | 9.00 | 5.08 | 4.35 | 31 |
| Time-indexed (24) | central | 10.05 | 5.67 | 4.86 | 38 |
| Time-indexed (24) | pessimistic | 11.73 | 6.61 | 5.67 | 51 |

MDDs are at the strictest Holm step (one-sided alpha 0.05 / 3) with 80% power. For the design control (one comparison,
alpha 0.05), the MDD at N = 30 is 3.3, 3.7 and 4.4 points. The time-indexed family is not powered at 5 points under the
central scenario, and this file says so.

## Budget

Every paid call runs in a GBEV-3 paid window, behind the metering proxy and the budget ledger, which reserves each
request before it is sent. The ledger cap is a runaway stop: reaching it pauses the run until GBEV-3 extends the cap,
and it does not end the test incomplete. A paused run resumes from its receipts without re-asking a question that was
already answered. Amounts are kept on GST-2010, not here.

## Risks

- **Ceiling.** The servers scored 58 of 60 on simple updates in October. S2 to S5 exist for that reason, and stage 1's
  R61 rule raises the ceiling once.
- **SD.** The per-world SD is modelled, not measured. The pilot measures it.
- **Rendering yield.** Stage 1 measures it.
- **The clock.** A run that slips more than 14 days past the world end regenerates the dates in a dated amendment.
- **Custody host.** BG1's waves run after RS1's, and paid windows run one at a time across the crew.

## What it can and can't claim

**If gbrain is ahead of a comparator:** "On synthetic histories with an equal mix of five kinds of change, gbrain at the
stable tag, reading 8,000 tokens, answers questions about current values more accurately than <comparator>, read by
Opus 5.5, GPT-6.1 Sol and particle-1." The design-control result and the per-kind rows sit beside it.

**If gbrain is behind:** the same sentence with "less accurately", in the same place.

**It does not say:**

- anything about brains where pages carry access flags (gbrain's private-by-default posture hides gold pages on real
  brains, a known weakness outside this test; these worlds carry no access flags and gbrain runs its single-principal
  facts posture);
- anything about agent mode, where gbrain's `as_of` and `known_as_of` tools would count;
- anything about histories much longer than 200,000 tokens, other token budgets or other gbrain pins;
- how the comparators rank against each other.

The claims read "on synthetic histories". The gap map's predictive-validity check against live recall on the real
brain is the named follow-up. A result favourable to particle-1 keeps its gap to Opus and the interval in the headline.

## Amendments

### Amendment 1 (2026-11-09): stage 1's cap

Stage 1's cap was its estimate divided by 0.75, rounded up to the dollar, under a fixed ceiling. It is now the larger
of two amounts, each rounded up to the dollar, under the same ceiling: the estimate divided by 0.75, and the sum of
stage 1's smoke leases.

Why: each stage 1 unit runs as a smoke unit, and a smoke unit leases twice its estimate. So the old cap could not hold
the leases that BG1's launch check sums against it, and the check would refuse stage 1 before any unit booted. The
counted block's cap already takes the larger of its estimate rule and its units' leases, and stage 1's now does too.

GBEV-3 granted this amendment on 2026-11-09, before any stage 1 cell ran.

### Amendment 2 (2026-11-09): stage 1's dev-world rendering

Stage 1's first real render of `bg1-dev-01` dropped the world at session 10 of 154, which failed its render and all
three re-renders. The run made 41 render attempts and 23 checks. Render attempts failed the length check 15 times, the
turn-format check 6 times and the date check (a calendar date written out instead of a date token) 3 times, and 3
claude-haiku-5-5 check replies came back empty. The cached replies show why. gemini-3.8-flash, at low thinking, wrote
sessions of about 450 to 1,500 tokens against targets of 757 to 3,236. Five of the turn-format failures were broken
JSON, such as a turn missing its `speaker` key, because the request asked for JSON with no schema. The empty Haiku
replies averaged about 210 of their 256 output tokens and held no text.

From commit `2c7ab96f`, rendering changes its method in these ways:

- Each render request gives Gemini a JSON Schema (`generationConfig.responseJsonSchema`): an object whose `turns` list
  holds turns with a `speaker` of `user` or `assistant` and a `content` string. Gemini's check asks for
  `{"extra_claims": boolean}` the same way.
- The render prompt states each session's length in words beside its token target: the target times 0.75, rounded to
  the nearest ten. It also asks for a minimum number of alternating turns: two per exchange, with one exchange (a
  casual message and a brief answer, about 190 words) per 250 target tokens, at least one exchange more than the
  session's planned lines, and never fewer than two exchanges.
- When a session fails the length check, its next render names the last attempt's measured length, in words and
  tokens, and the range the check allows, in words.
- The claude-haiku-5-5 check may write up to 2,048 output tokens instead of 256. Gemini's check keeps 256.
- Replies are cached under a new version, so no reply cached before this change is reused. Keyless (`--fake`)
  rendering is unchanged.

Nothing that judges a session moves: every check and the length check's range, the three re-renders per session, and
the rule that drops a world with a session that still fails (two drops among the first five mint slots pause minting).

### Amendment 3 (2026-11-09): stage 1's rendering cap

Stage 1's dev-world rendering runs under its own cap, beside the campaign cap. GBEV-3 raised the rendering cap on
