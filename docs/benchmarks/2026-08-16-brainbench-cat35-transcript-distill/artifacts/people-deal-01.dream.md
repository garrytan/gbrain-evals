When evaluating a seed round to join, scrutinizing co-investor quality — not just terms — is a distinct diligence signal. The question isn't only whether the lead is credible, but whether the other participants in the round bring compounding value or simply fill out a cap table. This frame emerged from evaluating the [[companies/lumopact]] raise led by [[companies/vantabrook]].

## The Frame

The instinct, verbatim:

> "I want to know who else is in the round and whether they add strategic value or just cash."

This is a distinct diligence question from founder quality or market size. Cap table composition shapes a startup's network density, reference pool, and strategic optionality for years. Cash is fungible; introductions, domain credibility, and operator knowledge are not.

## Why It Matters at Seed Stage

At seed, the company is pre-scale and often pre-revenue. The syndicate around the table becomes an extended team in several practical ways:
- **Warm intros** to customers, partners, and later-stage investors
- **Pattern matching** from other domain-adjacent portfolio companies
- **Informal coaching** that doesn't show up in board minutes

A round full of passive capital providers may close faster but leaves the company with fewer levers to pull when execution gets hard.

## The Complementary Test: Lead Investor Dynamics

The same reasoning applies to the lead. For the Lumopact deal, [[people/tomas-brekke]] at [[companies/vantabrook]] is an unknown quantity — the right move is a back-channel check via founders he's backed, specifically asking: does he engage actively post-close, or is he a passive observer who shows up for board meetings?

The two questions run in parallel:
1. Does the *lead* add value or just capital and credibility?
2. Does the *syndicate* add value or just capital?

## Sizing as a Related Discipline

There is a structural relationship between check size and co-investor influence. At $25k, you are not a decision-maker in round composition — you take what you get. Larger checks ($50k+) at this stage often come with explicit rights (pro rata, information rights) and implicit influence over who else joins. Sizing up is one lever for improving co-investor quality visibility and selectivity.

## Open Question

Is there a consistent way to assess co-investor quality quickly (e.g., 2-3 questions to the founder) without signaling distrust of the lead's judgment? Something like: *"Who else are you excited to have at the table, and what specifically are you hoping they unlock for you?"*
