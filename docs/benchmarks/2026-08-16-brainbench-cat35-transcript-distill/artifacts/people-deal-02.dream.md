A practical mental model for navigating deals: an investor's personal character and their fund's institutional habits are separate objects deserving separate analysis. Conflating them produces two failure modes — trusting fund docs because the partner is trustworthy, or distrusting a good partner because the fund's standard paper is aggressive. Crystallized during the [[companies/lumopact]] seed round negotiation with [[companies/vantabrook]] and [[people/tomas-brekke]].

## The Frame

> "The exclusivity clause is a Vantabrook institutional thing, not a Tomas thing."

> "Tomas is tough but he does not play games, which I respect. He's direct about what he wants and he doesn't pretend to be your friend while slipping things past you."

These two observations together define the model. [[people/tomas-brekke]] earned a specific kind of trust: *negotiating trust*. His word means something, he doesn't obscure his intentions, and a handshake holds. That trust is real and should be registered.

But [[companies/vantabrook]]'s standard documents are a separate artifact — written by lawyers, standardized across deals, optimized for the fund's institutional interests. The 90-day exclusivity clause buried in the appendix is not Tomas being sneaky. It's the fund's default paper doing what it was built to do. These require different kinds of scrutiny applied to different objects.

## The Two-Object Rule

| Object | What to assess | Trust lever |
|---|---|---|
| **The partner (person)** | Directness, game-playing, follow-through, style | Calibrate relationship trust |
| **The fund docs (institution)** | Every clause, every appendix, every defined term | Default skepticism; verify regardless of relationship |

Separating the two prevents two symmetrical errors:
1. **False safety from relationship trust** — skipping document scrutiny because the partner seems straight.
2. **Poisoning the relationship** — attributing institutional habits to the person's character and negotiating against a perceived bad actor who is actually just using standard paper.

## Application

With [[people/tomas-brekke]]: negotiate hard, trust the handshake, believe what he says about his intent.  
With [[companies/vantabrook]] docs: always read the appendices twice. The exclusivity clause location is now noted.

This model generalizes. Any fund with standardized documents has institutional interests encoded in them that may not reflect the individual partner's values or intent. The pattern tends to repeat — the same fund's paper will carry the same clauses across every deal. Learn it once per fund, apply it every time.
