# Multi-hop questions with held-out wording (N9, 2026-10-01)

At gbrain `d44296c` the composed results below are unchanged: 0 multi-relation plans and 0 firings. "Who attended <meeting>?" seeds now resolve (150 of 150 runs, was 0). The arm still fires 0 times, because world-v1 names attendees only in prose, which gbrain's evidence gate now types as mentions. The one-hop paid rerun repeated these numbers. ## The finding

gbrain's relationship retrieval does not help two- and three-hop questions, because it never runs on them. On 125 composed questions over world-v1 ("Who founded the companies that Alice Example invested in?"), each asked in a canonical and a paraphrase wording over three ingestion orders, the relationship arm fired on 0 of 750 runs per search path. Turning it on changed no result, so the on and off scores are identical: strict all-hit at 10 (every supporting page and every answer page in the first 10 results) was 10 of 375 runs (2.7%) for canonical wording and 5 of 375 (1.3%) for paraphrases on the keyless keyword path, and 3 of 375 (0.8%) for each wording on hybrid search with OpenAI embeddings. Under the preregistered rule this is "no demonstrated benefit" in every cell. It is the expected result: gbrain's parser handles one relation per question, so composition is a recorded feature gap (N9-1), not a failure.

The one-hop paid rerun that the 2026-09-29 report left open now has an answer. At the wave pin, relationship retrieval helps reworded one-hop questions: on the 145 paraphrased questions, recall at five rose from 0.411 to 0.537 and first-place hits from 4.8% to 16.6%, with 19 distinct questions better and none worse on recall (exact sign test p = 0.000004). At `b80cad6` the same split was flat (0 of 435 runs fired). The template split is unchanged from 2026-09-29: recall at five 0.737 to 0.763 and first-place hits 27.8% to 42.8%. The paraphrase split is development data (it was inspected on 2026-09-29 and the parser was widened afterwards), so this is evidence about the widened parser on known wording, not a held-out result.

The category also found three gbrain bugs against stated contracts, all of which keep relationship questions from reaching their answers: frontmatter relations that gbrain maps as incoming are stored backwards under the default schema pack (N9-2), attendance written in a meeting page's body is stored meeting to person instead of person to meeting (N9-3), and the seed of "Who attended <meeting>?" can never resolve (N9-4). Together they explain the open TODO "why relationship retrieval never fires on who attended questions": 0 of 150 attendance runs fired in either wording.

Measured on gbrain `3a284aea26889b77c633aebb4149c3016d834ee6` (v0.60.26.0), loaded through a copied overlay, with System One off (keys stripped, fresh `GBRAIN_HOME`). Report-only category: no number here gates CI.

## The concrete case

A composed question chains two or three typed relations. "Who founded the companies that Chris Jackson invested in?" needs the invested-in edges from the investor to the companies, then the founded edges from those companies to their founders. gbrain's relationship parser returns one relation per question. For that wording it returns no parse at all; for "Who founded the companies that Beth Williams advises?" it returns the founded relation with the seed "companies that Beth Williams advises". The resolver cannot map that phrase to a page, so the arm drops it and ordinary search answers alone.

## The experiment and results

**Questions.** `eval/generators/n9-multihop-paraphrase-gen.ts` builds 125 questions in seven families from world-v1's generator-written `_facts`: five two-hop families (investor to founders, advisor to founders, founder to investors, co-investors, meeting to investors; 94 questions) and two three-hop families (founder to the other portfolio companies of their investors, investor to the meetings their founders attended; 31 questions). Each has a canonical frame and one of three paraphrase frames, picked by seed 20261001. Gold is derived from the `_facts` chains, never from gbrain: answers, support (the pages whose facts state each edge) and required pages (support plus answers, at most 10 per question so all-hit at 10 is reachable). Anchors are fictional people, startups and meetings only. The file (`eval/data/n9-multihop-paraphrase-v1/questions.json`, SHA-256 `1a2bdd9c…def396`) was committed in `9a0603a` and the promotion rules in `5127c56`, both before the first scoring run; the runner refuses a file that differs from the generator. One disclosure: a plumbing check before the grammar was written ran "Who founded the companies that Chris Jackson invested in?", which matches one later canonical question; it showed no parse and no firing.

**Arms.** `eval/runner/n9-multi-hop-paraphrase.ts` reuses relational-ab's shared-index core: one PGLite index per ingestion seed (1, 2, 3; import plus link and timeline extraction), every question run with relationship retrieval off and on over the same index, the relational-ab pins (balanced mode, no reranker, no expansion, no autocut, graph signals on, depth 2), 10 result rows per composed question. The hermetic arm uses keyword search with no embedding provider; the paid arm adds OpenAI `text-embedding-3-large` (1,536 dimensions) with one shared query vector per question.

## gbrain bugs found

Each has a minimal keyless repro in `docs/benchmarks/2026-10-01-n9-multi-hop/repro-relational-edges.ts` (four fictional pages) and an entry in the [wave bug ledger](2026-10-01-wave-bugs.md).

1. **N9-2, frontmatter incoming relations stored backwards.** `extractFrontmatterLinks` (`src/core/link-extraction.ts:1724`) builds every schema-pack frontmatter mapping with direction `outgoing`. The default pack declares company `investors`, `key_people` and `partner`, deal `investors` and `lead`, and meeting `attendees`, which the in-code map (`:1333-1344`) defines as incoming. Expected: `investors: [people/bob-example]` on a company stores Bob to the company. Actual: company to Bob, so "Who invested in X?" (direction in) finds nothing.
2. **N9-3, meeting-body attendance stored meeting to person.** In `typeFor` (`src/core/link-extraction.ts:665-671`) the default pack's page-type-bound `attended` verb is returned before the canonical-attendance branch, without the `canonicalAttendance` flag, so `orientCanonicalAttendance` never runs. Expected: `Attendees: [Alice Example](people/alice-example)` on a meeting stores Alice to the meeting (what `extractPageLinks` produces without a pack). Actual: meeting to Alice; a fanout into the meeting returns nothing.
3. **N9-4, the attended seed never resolves.** The parser emits "Who attended X?" with a meeting as X, but `resolveSeedScoped` (`src/core/search/relational-recall.ts:94-104`) uses `resolveEntitySlugWithSource`, which returns entity pages only, so the seed falls to `fallback_slugify` and the arm drops it. Expected: the exact meeting title resolves to the meeting page. Actual: `seeds_resolved: 0` on every attendance question.

## Documented limits (not bugs)

- **N9-1, no composed query plans.** The parser maps one regex archetype to one relation set; composed questions are scored on what search returns anyway.
- **N9-5, bare one-word seeds that collide.** "Who works at Acme?" does not resolve when "Acme Labs" also exists, as the resolver documents (`src/core/entities/resolve.ts:13-21`); 15 of 145 one-hop template questions are affected.
- Superseding the old split: the 2026-09-29 paraphrase split is development data. The composed grammar here is held out from parser work: nobody changing the relational parser should read `questions.json`.

## What to use and what to avoid

Relationship retrieval is now worth enabling for ordinary one-hop wording about investors, advisors and employers; it gained on reworded questions and lost on none. Do not expect it to help questions that chain relations, or attendance questions, until composition exists and N9-2 to N9-4 are fixed. A fix for N9-2 to N9-4 should move the one-hop attendance rows; a composed-plan feature would show up first in this category's funnel (parsed, seed resolved, fired) and then in strict all-hit.
