# Short first names in full-name search (gbrain#7471): synthetic name-lookup eval

Written 2026-10-24 for GBRA-156. This mirrors the verdict for gbrain [#7472](https://github.com/garrytan/gbrain/pull/7472), the fix for [#7471](https://github.com/garrytan/gbrain/issues/7471). It compares gbrain master `3f0b6eecd` (v0.60.426.0) with the PR branch `ad136eeb3` on a synthetic brain. Every name is a placeholder.

**Verdict: ship.** On keyword-only search and on hybrid search with the reranker off, a full-name query that uses the other form of the given name went from 0 of 12 to 12 of 12 at rank 1, for example "Matt X" finding the page titled "Matthew X", or "Robert X" finding "Bob X". In every configuration, no query ranked worse on the branch. With the Voyage reranker on, both trees return the same result, the person page at rank 2 behind a company page that names the short form word for word. BrainBench (`gbrain eval run-all --suites brainbench`) is identical on all 24 cells. This is development evidence on a synthetic corpus built for the bug, not a held-out confirmation.

## What the fix does

- **Given-name variants in the title arm.** A 2–4 word query whose given name is in gbrain's nickname groups also searches each variant as its own websearch query, and the title boost counts a variant title match.
- **Rarity in the title arm's any-word retry.** For queries of up to 4 words where no title holds every word, titles that hold a rare word come first. Before, a title holding only a common word tied with them, and page id broke the tie.

## Corpus and queries

[`gen.py`](2026-10-24-name-variants-7471/gen.py) writes 560 pages:

- 12 given-name groups, each with:
  - a target person with a rare surname; half the targets are titled with the formal given name and half with the short form;
  - 15 short-form and 5 formal namesakes with common surnames;
  - 10 emails naming the short form and surname;
  - one company page naming both forms.
- 4 "rare word" notes next to 40 weekly-sync notes each.
- 12 non-name notes. Some open with a word that is also a nickname, like "Bill payment schedule" and "Art history reading list".

[`build.sh`](2026-10-24-name-variants-7471/build.sh) creates the target pages last, so they hold the highest page ids, as on a brain where the namesakes came first. The 64 queries fall into these families:

- **other-given-form:** the form the title doesn't use;
- **title-given-form:** the form the title uses;
- **surname-only;**
- **titled-name:** "Dr" plus the other form;
- **rare-word-or-fallback:** "Acme Quokka" style;
- **non-name-control.**

[`run.sh`](2026-10-24-name-variants-7471/run.sh) runs `gbrain search --limit 10 --json` for each query. [`summ.py`](2026-10-24-name-variants-7471/summ.py) scores rank 1, top 5 and MRR.

## Reading the gaps

- **Keyword-only rare-word family stays at 0.** The title arm now ranks the rare-word title first; gbrain's `title-arm-exact` test pins that on PGLite and Postgres. End to end, though, the keyword arm's own any-word fallback still ranks every "Acme" note above it. That fallback is the next GBRA-156 wave.
- **Reranker on.** The cross-encoder puts the company page that contains "Matt X" verbatim first and the person page second on both trees. On this small brain, the vector arm already brought the person page into the rerank pool on master. The production report's miss (the person page absent from the top 5) needs the page to be outside that pool, which is the keyword-only shape here.
- **Titled names ("Dr Matt X")** improve keyword-only only to hit@5 0.50. The title arm requires "dr" in the title, and variants don't strip honorifics.

## BrainBench

`gbrain eval run-all --suites brainbench` (seed 42) on both trees: 24 cells, 0 differing. The cells are in [`brainbench.json`](2026-10-24-name-variants-7471/brainbench.json).

## What would change this

- A held-out name set from a real brain's display names (sender and attendee resolution) would be needed to claim a production rate.
- A brain large enough that the vector arm misses the person page would test the reranker-on path for the pool-entry miss directly.
