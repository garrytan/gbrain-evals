## The plan's decision A (verbatim)

**A. Probe first (Garry's A1).** 1) After Phase 1's task sets and before any adapter, run a headroom probe on all three skills at today's pin, on the selection split only, for about $20:
- no skill and the seed, 3 runs per task;
- a one-shot rewrite through gbrain's exported `runSkillOpt` one-shot mode, given a benchmark file of the 24 train tasks only (split `[1,22,1]`, so its forward pass and criteria come from train), two repeats, then scored on selection by the harness and kept only if it beats the seed;
- the seed on Opus 5.5, 1 run per task.

The harness scores selection with its own counted scorer through `scoreSkillOnTasks`' `scoreFn` seam. Before any paid call, a keyless check lists each skill's frontmatter tools that today's rollout does not serve (today: `recall` and `get_timeline` for `query`, `get_timeline` for `briefing`), and the probe reports them as a harness limit. A rewrite on such a skill can look like it took all the gain by routing around missing tools, so a stop verdict may rest only on skills with full coverage; if it would depend on a skill with a gap, that skill's probe reruns after the wave serves its tools (about $5), and the stop waits for the rerun.

## Question

Do gbrain's shipped `query`, `briefing` and `fact-check` skills leave room that a skill optimizer could show, at the pinned gbrain? Or does one Opus rewrite already take all the gain these tasks can measure? The answer decides whether the rollout server (plan Phase 2) and the outside optimizer arms (Phase 3) get built.

## Metric and denominator

An answer is correct only when every check of its task passes: each gold value matches a tolerant pattern (separators, units, "k", any common date format with the year, any case for names), a gold page is cited in the skill's own form (`briefing`: `[Source: slug, updated DATE]`; `query` and `fact-check`: the slug), `fact-check` gives a `STATUS:` the evidence supports and none it rules out, and a negative control abstains while naming what was asked about. The arm's selection score is the mean over the 24 selection tasks of the median of its runs (gbrain's `scoreSkillOnTasks`, with this scorer in its `scoreFn` seam). A rollout that ends badly (turn limit, refusal, truncation) is scored on its final text. An error gbrain treats as must-abort leaves the arm unscored, and the gate then reads `incomplete`. Citation nDCG is recorded and never counted.

The solvability ceiling is the share of selection tasks whose solvability control passes: 1.00 for all three skills. One task is 0.042 of a selection score.

## Decision rule

A skill qualifies when its seed scores from 0.20 to 0.80 on selection and its kept one-shot score is at least 0.15 below the ceiling.

1. **Build** (Phase 2, then Phase 3) when at least two skills qualify.
2. Otherwise, when one-shot is within 0.15 of the ceiling on a skill that does not qualify: **harden the tasks once and probe again**. The hardening is a dated amendment to this file, written before the second probe, which runs with `--after-hardening`.
3. After hardening, the same result is a **stop**: report to Garry that one rewrite captures the measurable gain on gbrain's shipped skills, and carry the loop's CUT-CANDIDATE forward.
4. A stop may rest only on skills whose frontmatter brain operations the rollout serves in full. A gap is a frontmatter tool that is a gbrain operation the rollout does not serve: today `recall` and `get_timeline` for `query`, and `get_timeline` for `briefing`, which the gbrain wave is asked to serve. A stop that rests on a skill with a gap waits for that skill's probe to rerun once the rollout serves those operations (about $5 per skill). `fact-check` declares `web_search` and `web_fetch`, which are not gbrain operations, so no gbrain rollout can serve them; no task uses its web lane, so they are listed apart (`outside_brain` in the receipt) and are not a gap. `fact-check` counts as fully covered.
5. When no skill qualifies only because seeds fall outside the band (no one-shot at the ceiling), the result is **out of band**. It goes to the Captain, and any retuning of the tasks is an amendment.

The seed-on-Opus score is reported beside the gate and does not gate. The gate compares point estimates against fixed thresholds; it is a build decision, not a significance test, so no multiplicity correction applies. Any null or negative result gets one "Why null?" cycle (exposure, task fit, power and saturation, frontier model) before it is reported.

## What each outcome changes

- Build: Phase 2 (rollout server and ledger) starts, then Phase 3's arms and the pilot.
- Harden and reprobe: one amendment and one more probe (about $20); nothing else is built.
- Stop: no server or outside arm is built. The report goes to Garry through the Captain, with the stop held until any gap skill's rerun.
- Out of band: the Captain decides whether to retune the tasks.

**Analysis.** An arm's score is the mean over tasks of the median of its runs. Two differences are computed per pair: seed minus no skill, and rewrite minus seed. Each is paired by task and gets:
- a 95% percentile interval from a task-level bootstrap (10,000 resamples, seed 799);
- the counts of tasks won and lost;
- an exact two-sided McNemar p on the discordant tasks.

Each arm also reports its cost per correct answer: metered USD at list price over correct rollouts.

**Verdicts, per target and skill.**
- **Shipped skill against no skill.** KEEP when the interval's lower bound is above 0 and McNemar p is below 0.05. CUT-CANDIDATE when the point difference is 0 or below. FIX-METHOD otherwise. Every FIX-METHOD and CUT-CANDIDATE gets one "Why null?" cycle in the report.
- **Rewrite against seed.** `rewrite-beats-seed` when the lower bound is above 0 and p is below 0.05. In that case the shipped seed is FIX-METHOD, and the rewrite goes to gbrain as a candidate. `rewrite-worse` when the upper bound is below 0 and p is below 0.05. `no-difference-shown` otherwise.

There are twelve comparisons, two per pair, and each verdict is read per pair. The report also gives Holm-adjusted p across all twelve and flags any verdict that does not survive it. A null here is read beside the selection ceiling. A rewrite that holds 1.00 on test and a seed that also scores near 1.00 is a saturation result, not evidence for either.
