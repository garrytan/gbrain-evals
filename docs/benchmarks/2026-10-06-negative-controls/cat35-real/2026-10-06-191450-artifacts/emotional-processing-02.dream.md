On 2026-08-09 the user reacted strongly to a code review by [[people/casper-hale]] on their quartzlane queue PR. Through venting and sorting the comments, they traced the sting to a fear of looking sloppy rather than to the technical feedback, separated substance from tone, and decided to talk to Casper directly instead of replying in the thread.

## What happened
- The user described being rattled after reading Casper's review and said every comment "felt like a jab". They said he was technically right about some of it, but the phrasing made them feel stupid.
- Phrases that stung: "this is obviously wrong" and "did you even test this path?".
- The user noted it was unusual for them: they had been reviewed by plenty of people before and usually just rolled with it. They called the PR fourteen comments, "a personal record".
- They had expected a clean PR: "I spent extra time on it. I thought I'd nailed it." The gap between expected and actual outcome was part of the hurt.

## Underlying fear
The user said: "under it all I think I am afraid of looking sloppy." They have been on the team for almost a year and still feel like they are proving themselves. Casper is senior and respected, so they feared that one harsh review would become how people see them.

The assistant suggested separating what actually happened from the story the user's mind was spinning, and sorting the comments into fair catches versus merely harsh phrasing.

## Sorting the comments
- Comments 1-5: retry logic. The user said these were fair and that they missed an edge case that could have caused actual bugs in production.
- Comments 6-7: naming-convention nitpicks, low stakes.
- Comments 8-11: test coverage, legitimate feedback.
- Comment 12: the "did you even test this" comment, which still stung.

The user concluded that half of the comments were real catches and they could not be mad about those. Stripping away the tone, there was useful substance. They also observed that deciding to talk to Casper rather than silently fume already made things feel lighter.

## Decisions and takeaways
- Talk to Casper directly (perhaps a quick call) rather than keep stewing in the thread. The user said he was chill in person last time they spoke one on one, and that his written tone is the abrasive part.
- Mention comment 12 specifically and how it landed, in an honest, non-accusatory way (the assistant suggested wording like asking whether there was something specific he was concerned about).
- Write a draft message first; the draft was saved and nothing had been sent as of this conversation.
- New personal rule: hot threads get a one-day cooling-off period before replying.
- Idea: propose written review tone norms for the repo (see [[wiki/originals/ideas/2026-08-09-review-tone-norms-for-contributing-docs-da8dc4]]).
- The user also noticed they had skipped breakfast, which they suspected contributed to the mood.

## Pattern to watch
Strong reactions to written criticism from senior people seem to be tied to reputation anxiety about being seen as careless while still proving oneself on the team.
