The user is exploring a startup idea: a calendar tool that automatically defragments itself around focus blocks, so meetings stop fragmenting deep-work time. They chose chiefs of staff as the first user, plan eight discovery interviews before writing code, and set a three-week validation window ending in a kill-or-commit decision. Advice from the angel investor [[people/mira-voss]] about distribution shaped the user choice.

## The idea

The user described the problem this way: "Every time I look at my week, meetings are scattered like shrapnel, and the two-hour chunk I need for deep work just... evaporates." They believe nobody is solving this well.

## Wedge: internal one-on-ones

The user said the wedge is internal one on ones, because nobody defends those slots. External meetings carry social pressure, while skip-levels and casual syncs land wherever the algorithm drops them. The assistant noted that because both participants work at the same company, only one organization has to buy in. The assistant floated reclaiming four to six contiguous hours a week per person as a possible ROI story; this was the assistant's illustration, not a measured figure.

## First user: chiefs of staff

The user's reasoning: chiefs of staff already wrangle exec calendars, feel the pain directly, and have enough clout to trial something without a six-month procurement cycle. The user also said a founder might love it but won't evangelize, whereas a chief of staff manages five or six calendars and talks to peers at other companies.

## Distribution concern from Mira

The user reported that [[people/mira-voss]] keeps saying scheduling dies on distribution, not product, and that the graveyard is full of smart scheduling apps nobody adopted. Her suggested antidote, as relayed by the user: pick a user who already aggregates calendars for others, so you get leverage. The assistant suggested the distribution path would be word of mouth in informal chief-of-staff Slack groups, plus the visibility of protected blocks to anyone booking the exec.

## Positioning

The user's draft one-liner: "I'm building a tool that automatically defragments your exec's calendar so focus time stays intact." The assistant proposed the framing of a calendar janitor (low ego, high trust, delegated cleanup). The user responded: "Calendar janitor... I like that framing. Feels humble and specific."

## Biggest risk named by the user

The user judged trust to be real but API limits manageable for the major providers. The deeper risk: manually defragging their own calendar bought back six hours a week, but they wondered whether they are an outlier. They said that if the average savings is only one hour, the value prop collapses.

The assistant suggested testing this in interviews by having chiefs of staff walk through last week's calendar and identify meetings that drifted into focus blocks. Per the assistant, clustering around three-plus hours reclaimed would be signal.

## Validation plan (decided by the user)

- Eight interviews, quantify the drift, then decide.
- Three weeks of validation, then a kill or commit call.
- If commit, show Mira a sketch once the interviews wrap.
- The user has three warm intros from an old ops community and aims to have all eight scheduled by end of week one, then run them back to back. The user called the timeline "Tight but doable."
- The assistant suggested splitting into week one for sourcing and scheduling and weeks two and three for running calls and synthesis, ending each call with an ask for one more referral, and bringing Mira a one-pager.

No interviews had been completed in this conversation, and no decision had been made yet.

The user is excited about a new startup idea, a self-defragmenting calendar aimed at chiefs of staff, and noted that this excitement is unusual for them. They are deliberately pairing it with a disciplined validation process because of past experience building too early. The angel investor [[people/mira-voss]] is a feedback source they feel they owe a sharper version to.

## What the user said about their state

The user said: "Honestly, this one makes me a little giddy, which past ideas never did. I've been burned before by building too early, so I'm trying to stay disciplined."

## How discipline shows up

- They want eight chief of staff interviews "before I write a line of code."
- They set a three-week validation window followed by a kill or commit call, rather than open-ended exploration.
- They named their own key assumption as a risk: the six hours a week they reclaimed by manually defragging their own calendar may make them an outlier, and if average savings is only one hour, the value prop collapses.
- They plan to show [[people/mira-voss]] a sketch only if the decision is commit, saying she has been generous with feedback and they owe her a sharper version.

## Pattern worth noting

The user pre-commits to a kill option and questions their own sample of one, which looks like a guard against the earlier pattern of building too early. This is an observation from the transcript, not an outcome: the interviews had not yet been run.
