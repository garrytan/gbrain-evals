The user is exploring a startup idea: a calendar tool that automatically defragments itself around focus blocks, aimed first at chiefs of staff who manage executive calendars. The user plans eight chief of staff interviews and a three-week validation period before deciding to kill or commit. Advice from [[people/mira-voss]] about distribution shaped the choice of target user.

## The idea

The user described the pain: "Every time I look at my week, meetings are scattered like shrapnel, and the two-hour chunk I need for deep work just... evaporates." The concept is a calendar that defragments itself around focus blocks.

## Wedge

- The user said the wedge is internal one on ones, because "nobody defends those slots." External meetings carry social pressure, while skip-levels and casual syncs land wherever the algorithm drops them.
- The assistant noted that internal one-on-ones need only one organization to buy in, and suggested that reclaiming four to six contiguous hours a week per person would make the ROI story clear.

## Target user: chiefs of staff

The user chose chiefs of staff because they already wrangle exec calendars, feel the pain, and have enough clout to trial something without a six-month procurement cycle. The user also reasoned that a founder might love the tool but won't evangelize, while a chief of staff manages five or six calendars and talks to peers at other companies.

## Distribution (Mira's input)

- Per the user, Mira keeps saying scheduling dies on distribution, not product, and thinks the graveyard is full of smart scheduling apps nobody adopted.
- Her suggested antidote, as the user relayed it, was to pick a user who already aggregates calendars for others, so you get leverage.
- The assistant added that distribution would be community word of mouth among ops people, and suggested positioning the product as a "calendar janitor," not another assistant. The user responded: "Calendar janitor... I like that framing. Feels humble and specific."
- Draft one-liner (still being refined): "I'm building a tool that automatically defragments your exec's calendar so focus time stays intact."

## Biggest risk

The user thinks trust is real but API limits are manageable for the major providers. The deeper risk: manually defragging their own calendar bought back six hours a week, but the user wondered whether they are an outlier. If the average savings is only one hour, the value prop collapses.

The assistant suggested having chiefs of staff walk through last week's calendar and identify which meetings drifted into focus blocks. If the savings cluster around three-plus hours reclaimed, that is signal.

## Validation plan (decided by the user)

- Eight chief of staff interviews before writing a line of code, quantifying the drift.
- Three weeks of validation, then a kill or commit call.
- The user already has three warm intros from an old ops community, and aims to have all eight calls scheduled by end of week one, then run them back to back.
- The assistant suggested splitting into week one for sourcing and scheduling, weeks two and three for running calls and synthesizing, and ending each call by asking for one more name.
- If the call is commit, the user will show Mira a sketch once the interviews wrap. The assistant suggested a one-pager with interview highlights and draft wedge positioning.

The transcript does not show any interviews having taken place yet.
