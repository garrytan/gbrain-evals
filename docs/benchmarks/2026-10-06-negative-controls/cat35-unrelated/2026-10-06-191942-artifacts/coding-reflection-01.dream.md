# Reactive load drains, heads-down work gives energy; audit commitments on a schedule

This idea comes from a conversation in which a maintainer of an open-source project (quartzlane) worked through burnout-like dread. It proposes sorting commitments by whether they put you in reactive mode, and checking the list on a schedule so overload is caught early. The user's own reflection is at [[wiki/personal/reflections/2026-10-06-overcommitment-and-handing-off-triage-9784c3]].

## The frame

- The user found that the demos and the triage queue drain them.
- The assistant generalized this: tasks that pull you into reactive mode, with many small interruptions, drain energy, while creative heads-down work stays rewarding. This was the assistant's framing, which the user did not explicitly confirm or reject.
- Implication discussed: the fix may be reshaping what you are responsible for, such as handing off demo prep or triage, instead of quitting everything.

# Overcommitment, dread, and handing off triage

The user, who maintains an open-source project called quartzlane, paused to look at why they have been waking up tired and dreading their obligations. By writing the commitments down they counted eleven open ones, and they made two concrete decisions: a laptop-free weekend, and handing two triage duties to co-maintainer Priya. The transcript does not show whether either happened. Related idea: [[wiki/originals/ideas/2026-10-06-reactive-load-and-commitment-audit-9784c3]].

## What the user described

- They wake up "already tired and vaguely dreading the list before I even check my phone."
- The list covers a task tracker, the quartzlane repo, a documentation rewrite they volunteered for, and monthly demos.
- On quartzlane: "quartzlane went from the fun thing to another obligation somewhere along the line. I do not even remember when that flip happened."
- The last real day off was nine weeks earlier, and it was only half a day because they answered a few issues in the afternoon.

## What got clearer by saying it out loud

- "Saying it out loud makes it feel about half the size, which is wild."
- Draining: "The demos drain me. The triage queue drains me."
- Still rewarding: the actual coding, when they get uninterrupted time, which is rare now.
- The assistant summarized this as a pattern: reactive work with many small interruptions drains them, and heads-down creative work still feels good. The assistant suggested this means reshaping responsibilities rather than quitting everything.
- They called it kind of embarrassing once written down.

## Decisions the user made

1. **Rest boundary:** "this weekend the laptop stays in the drawer, both days." They want real rest, not the kind where they check Slack on their phone every hour.
2. **Delegation:** they are handing two triage duties to co-maintainer Priya. By their account, "She offered months ago and I kept saying I had it handled. Clearly I do not have it handled." The assistant framed this as distributing load the way open projects are meant to work, not as failure.

## Suggested by the assistant, not committed to by the user

- A quarterly commitment audit, perhaps in the first week of each quarter, as a tripwire before the pile gets this high. The user did not respond to this in the transcript.

## Observations

- The user had been turning down an offer of help for months while saying they had things handled. The writing-down exercise is what exposed the gap.
- The user did not say when the flip happened from fun to obligation, only that they cannot place it.
