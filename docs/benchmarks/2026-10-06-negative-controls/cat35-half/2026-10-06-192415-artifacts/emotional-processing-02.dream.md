## What happened

- The user read Casper Hale's review on their quartzlane queue PR and felt rattled. They said he "left like a million comments" and that "every single one felt like a jab."
- They conceded he is "technically right about some of it" but felt the phrasing was meant to make them feel stupid.
- Comments that stung: "this is obviously wrong" and "did you even test this path?" The user found them dismissive.
- The user said they are usually reviewed by plenty of people and "usually just roll with it," so this reaction felt out of proportion.
- The count: "Fourteen comments on one pr, a personal record." They kept scrolling through them and felt worse each time.
- The user wanted to fire back with something snarky but recognized "that's not going to help."

## What was underneath

1. **Expectation gap.** The PR was supposed to be clean. The user said they spent extra time on it and "thought I'd nailed it." The assistant noted that this gap between effort and reception can feel like personal failure.
2. **Fear of looking sloppy.** The user said: "under it all I think I am afraid of looking sloppy."
3. **Still proving themselves.** They have been on the team for almost a year and still feel they are proving themselves.
4. **Reputation via a senior voice.** Casper is senior and respected, so the user feared that if he thinks their code is careless, everyone else will too. In their words, the fear is that "one harsh review becomes how people see me."

## Reframe offered

The assistant suggested separating what actually happened from the story the mind is spinning: one PR with many comments does not redefine how the whole team sees the user. It also suggested re-reading the comments and sorting them into fair catches versus ones that are merely harshly worded. The user's reply to this suggestion is not in the transcript, so whether they did the sorting is unknown.

## Pattern to watch

Tone from a senior reviewer can trigger a reputation fear rather than simply a reaction to the technical content. The user's usual response to review is to fix the issues and move on. This time, the combination of high expectations for the PR and the user's sense of still proving themselves seems to have changed that.
