The user is exploring a startup idea: a calendar that automatically defragments itself around focus blocks, so meetings stop fragmenting the week and deep-work time is preserved. The plan centers on internal one-on-ones as the wedge and on chiefs of staff as the first users, with eight interviews planned before any code. The distribution-focused advice from [[people/mira-voss]] shaped the choice of user.

## The idea

The user described the problem this way: "Every time I look at my week, meetings are scattered like shrapnel, and the two-hour chunk I need for deep work just... evaporates." The user also believes "Nobody is solving this well." The core metaphor is defragmentation: the calendar is treated as broken by default, and the tool repairs it.

## Wedge: internal one-on-ones

The user said the wedge is internal one on ones because "nobody defends those slots." External meetings carry social pressure, while a skip-level or casual sync gets placed wherever it lands. The user wants to fight that drift. The assistant noted that both participants work at the same company, so only one organization has to buy in. It also suggested a proof point of reclaiming roughly four to six contiguous hours a week per person. That figure came from the assistant as an illustrative target, not a measured result.

## First user: chiefs of staff

The user's reasoning was: "They're already wrangling exec calendars, they feel the pain firsthand, and they have enough clout to trial something without a six-month procurement cycle." The user said, "I want to do eight chief of staff interviews before I write a line of code." Questions to validate in those interviews, per the assistant, are whether the pain is acute enough to pay for and whether chiefs of staff would trust software to move meetings on their behalf.

## Distribution concern and Mira's advice

 Her suggested antidote, as relayed by the user, was to pick a user who already aggregates calendars for others, which gives leverage. This is partly why the user chose chiefs of staff over individual founders: "A founder might love it but won't evangelize; a chief of staff manages five or six calendars and talks to peers at other companies."

The assistant added a possible distribution hook: if a chief of staff installs the tool for an exec, everyone who books that exec sees the protected blocks. It described this as a small distribution surface, but a start.
