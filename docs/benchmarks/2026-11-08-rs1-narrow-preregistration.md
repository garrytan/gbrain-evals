# Narrowed RS1 preregistration: is gbrain within 3.5 points of the two leading memory servers on new BEAM conversations?

Written on 2026-11-08 for GST-1886, PR 1 of two (R77: this preregistration and its harness, then a results PR). GBEV-3
chose this design, option 2b of GBEV-48's priced plan, on 2026-11-08, with a check-in line at a projected $2,300 of new
spend. Nothing has been minted for it, no RS1 conversation exists, and no RS1 cell has run. The custodian is GBRA-240,
named by GBRA-108 under its custody program. This file is frozen by PR 1's merge commit. Any later change is a dated
amendment under "Amendments", written before the cells it governs.

RS1 itself is not new. Its first design, 75 conversations ranking gbrain against three servers and a whole-system arm,
stopped at stage 0 on 2026-11-04 because it did not fit the $2,000 planning line per eval
([stage 0 finding](2026-11-04-rs1-stage0.md)). This file revives it as a narrower test with one claim.

- **Cells:** `eval/runner/q1/cells/rs1-cells.json`, generated by `bun eval/runner/q1/cells/rs1.ts` with Q1's tooling
  (`eval/runner/q1/cells/definitions.ts`) and F6's lease helpers. Every `--manifest` switch takes `rs1`: `plan`, `show`,
  `run`, `merge` and campaign units in `eval/runner/q1/cell.ts`, and `eval/runner/q1/reread-cell.ts`.
- **Decision rule:** `eval/runner/rs1/iut.ts`, called by both the power simulation and the verdict.
- **Analysis:** `eval/runner/rs1/verdict.ts`, with a `verdict` mode and a `pilot` mode. It reads cell and re-read
  receipts and prints JSON and a Markdown table. There is no scoreboard mode and no publication block.
- **Power and money:** `eval/runner/rs1/power.ts` and `eval/runner/rs1/pricing.ts` (writes `pricing.json`).
- **Custody:** `eval/custody/rs1/mint.sh`, `rs1_mint.py`, `validate.ts` and `hashlist.ts`, and the loader `beam-rs1` in
  `eval/runner/memory-qa/corpus.ts`.

## The question

On million-token conversations that no system and no gbrain contributor has seen, written by BEAM's own generator: is
gbrain at its stable release, reading 8,000 tokens of memory, at most 3.5 points behind each of the two leading memory
servers, under both BEAM's judge and frontier judges?

That is a non-inferiority question. It asks whether gbrain is in the leading group, not whether it is first.

## Why it matters

gbrain's main competitive claim is that it sits with the best memory servers. The evidence today is F6's BEAM-1M sealed
comparison, 24 conversations at gbrain `0ccf2bdd`. Under frontier judges F6 puts gbrain 2.0 points above the memory-bank
server (95% interval -1.4 to +5.4) and 3.5 points above the extract-first server (+0.4 to +6.5); under BEAM's judge the
gaps are +1.3 (-0.9 to +3.7) and +4.8 (+2.6 to +7.1)
([GST-1365 results](2026-11-05-gst-1365-judge-robustness.md), "Comparisons"). F6 preregistered these comparisons as
descriptive, and gbrain's settings (arm P) were chosen on that same set, at an old pin. So they describe the systems and
cannot be read as a tested claim.

The nearest rival also changes with the set. On BEAM-1M it is the memory-bank server. On BEAM-10M, never used for
tuning, it is the extract-first server (-0.3, interval -3.6 to +2.6 under frontier judges), and gbrain leads the
memory-bank server there by 4.9 points. A claim worth stating has to cover both. A fresh, sealed, preregistered test
against both servers is the smallest experiment that turns the description into a tested claim, at about $2,033 of new
spend.

## The set

**Size.** Up to 33 conversations in BEAM's 1M tier, about 28 planned, with 20 probing questions each: two for each of
BEAM's ten abilities (abstention, contradiction resolution, event ordering, information extraction, instruction
following, knowledge update, multi-session reasoning, preference following, summarization and temporal reasoning), in
BEAM's rubric format with graded scoring.

**Generator.** BEAM ([mohammadtavakoli78/BEAM](https://github.com/mohammadtavakoli78/BEAM), commit `b2da22e`), its
plan, question and answer stages run in custody by `eval/custody/rs1/mint.sh` with gemini-3.8-flash on a
billing-enabled Google project. Each conversation is truncated at BEAM's 1M-tier limit, and generation stops answering
once the chat passes that limit (stage 0's early stop, which left every file BEAM keeps byte-identical on c003). BEAM's
Python dependencies install with uv from a hash-pinned lock into an isolated Python 3.12 environment, and each paid step
runs behind its own metering proxy holding only that provider's key.

**Topics.** New topics come from a private seed in custody, with up to five attempts per conversation. A topic is
rejected when it matches one of the 90 public topics of BEAM-100K, 500K and 1M or an accepted RS1 topic: the same
normalized title, word overlap (Jaccard) of at least 0.6, or text-embedding-3-large cosine of at least 0.85
(`eval/custody/rs1/rs1_mint.py`).

### Mint slots, the yield gate and the kept set

The loader has 48 mint slots, `rs1-001` to `rs1-048` (`RS1_CONVERSATIONS` in `eval/runner/memory-qa/corpus.ts`, all
sealed in `eval/decisions/splits/beam-rs1.json`). 48 covers the cap of 33 at the planned yield of 0.85, with room.

1. **Slot order.** Conversations are minted and validated in slot order.
2. **Yield gate.** After the first 5 conversations are minted and validated, the custodian counts drops. If 2 or more of
   the 5 drop, minting pauses and the counts go to GBEV-3.
3. **Kept set.** The kept set is the first N_final slots that pass validation, in slot order. Minting stops as soon as
   the kept count reaches N_final, so no slot is minted past it and no conversation is chosen by anything other than
   slot order.
4. **Waves.** Wave A is the first 20 kept conversations. Wave B is the next N_final - 20, with N_final at most 33.
5. **The kept list.** After validation the custodian writes the exact kept list into the filled manifest
   (`eval/decisions/datasets/rs1-manifest.json`), in a `kept` field in slot order, and `n_final` once it is known. The
   runner refuses a kept id the filled manifest does not pin, an unfilled manifest, and a launch unit whose
   conversations are not yet all in the list.

### Validation

`eval/custody/rs1/validate.ts` runs unchanged from main. A conversation gate drops the conversation, and its items are
not checked:

- **Length:** its cl100k token count is outside 1,070,000 plus or minus 15%.
- **Contamination:** its largest share of distinct 13-grams found in any one chat or questions file of the 28 dev-split
  public BEAM conversations is 1% or more. Stage 0's c003 scored 0.00008 against that bar.
- **Duplicate:** the same chat bytes as an earlier kept conversation, or an estimated 13-gram Jaccard of at least 0.1
  with one.

Item gates apply to both items of each ability. An item fails when its slot is missing; when it duplicates an earlier
item (the same normalized question, or word 3-shingle Jaccard of at least 0.7); when it is contaminated (a public BEAM
question, or half or more of its 13-grams public); when none of the turns it cites is in the truncated chat; or when it
lacks support. Two validators from different families, gemini-3.8-flash and claude-haiku-5-5, must each find the
reference answer supported by the cited turns. An abstention item cites nothing, so both validators scan the whole
conversation in overlapping windows of 60,000 cl100k tokens, and the item fails if any window answers it.

**Per-ability floor.** A conversation keeps both items of every ability. A conversation with a failed item is
regenerated up to twice (three selections in all) and dropped after that. Regeneration re-picks failed items from BEAM's
own candidate pool and validates only the replacements; it does not re-mint. Validation stops (exit 4) when more than
20% of the items of finished conversations fail.

**Published.** Counts per ability, the token-length distribution, validation pass and drop counts by reason, and the
SHA-256 of the hash list. Nothing else leaves custody.

## Readers and judges

**Readers.** claude-opus-5-5 and gpt-6.1-sol read every row live, at effort medium, with Q1's output limit of 2,048
tokens. Each reads the same frozen 8,000-token context for a given system and question, and each context is written once
with its SHA-256. particle-1 re-reads every row's frozen contexts byte for byte, each checked against its recorded
SHA-256 (`eval/runner/q1/reread-cell.ts`), at effort medium with its 16,000-token output floor, in crew paid windows.
No reader is Sonnet, Haiku, Fable or gpt-5.4-mini. F6's three readers included Sonnet; RS1 has particle-1 in its place.

**Judges.** Every system-row answer gets two judges:

| Reader | Protocol judge (bridge) | Frontier judge |
|---|---|---|
| claude-opus-5-5 | gpt-4.1-mini | gpt-6.1-sol |
| particle-1 | gpt-4.1-mini | gpt-6.1-sol |
| gpt-6.1-sol | gpt-4.1-mini | claude-opus-5-5 |

- **gpt-4.1-mini** is BEAM's canonical judge. It is the protocol judge and the bridge to F6 and Q1, and its records use
  the instrument's canonical id.
- **The frontier judge** is picked by the reader's family, so no frontier judge grades its own family's answers
  (GST-767). Its records use `<instrument>@<judge>`.
- **The family rule binds the frontier judge.** The protocol judge is exempt, because a protocol's judge stays. That is
  why gpt-4.1-mini may grade Sol's answers.
- **particle-1 never judges.**
- **Scored separately.** The two judge views are scored separately everywhere and never averaged into one score.
- **The floor row** gets the protocol judge only.
- **Completeness.** A missing frontier judgment counts as unjudged for completeness and for resume, so a cell is not
  complete until both judges have graded every answer.

The cell definitions refuse a per-reader plan whose frontier judge shares the reader's family or is particle-1.

**Disclosed overlaps.** claude-haiku-5-5 is gbrain's extraction model and an item validator. gpt-4.1-mini is the
extract-first server's extraction model and the protocol judge. gemini-3.8-flash writes the gold answers and validates
items.

### The floor row: a named deviation

GBEV-48's design had gpt-4.1-mini read and judge the floor row. This preregistration has claude-opus-5-5 and gpt-6.1-sol
read it, with particle-1's re-read, and gpt-4.1-mini judge it, because AGENTS.md allows gpt-4.1-mini only as the bridge
judge. The floor is never part of a claim. verdict.ts reports it, and whether it sits below every system row under the
protocol judge, as a check that the set still needs memory (F6 put it about 35 points below every server).

## Estimand and verdict rules

The rules below are written once, in `eval/runner/rs1/iut.ts`. power.ts's simulation and verdict.ts both call it, and a
test gives both the same synthetic data and checks that they reach the same pass or fail.

### Primary: non-inferiority by intersection-union

1. **Question score.** For each question, a row's score under a judge view is the mean over the three readers (Opus and
   Sol live, particle-1 from its re-read, joined by question id) of that reader's BEAM graded score times 100.
2. **Conversation difference.** A conversation's difference is the mean over its common questions of gbrain minus the
   server.
3. **Statistic.** The mean of the conversation differences, with its one-sided 95% t lower bound on n - 1 degrees of
   freedom, where n is the number of conversations.
4. **Four tests.** gbrain minus the memory-bank server and gbrain minus the extract-first server, each under the
   frontier view and the bridge view.
5. **Pass.** The claim passes if and only if all four lower bounds are above -3.5 points. This is an
   intersection-union test, so alpha is not split.

**Why 3.5 points.** It is the margin Garry Tan approved on 2026-10-06 for this comparison (gbrain against the memory-bank
server at 8,000 tokens on BEAM, memory-proof wave 1), and README's wave-2 line uses it. GST-1365 found judge shifts of
-2.7 to +3.1 points on F6's comparisons, so gaps under about 3 points move with the judge alone. The memory-bank server
sits about 35 points above the floor on F6's BEAM-1M set, so 3.5 points is about 10% of its effect.

### Missing data

- **One common question set.** All four tests use the same questions. A question enters only when gbrain and both
  servers have a judged value from all three readers under both judge views.
- **Product failures score 0 and stay in.** The runner's outcome field classifies them: `retrieval_error`,
  `unsupported`, `turn_cap` and `context_overflow` (`PRODUCT_FAILURES` in `eval/runner/memory-qa/outcomes.ts`).
- **Harness failures drop the question.** `reader_error`, `judge_error`, `harness_invalid`, `budget_not_run`, or a
  missing judgment or re-read answer. Every drop is counted by reason and by side (gbrain or server).
- **Drop cap.** If more than 5% of a comparison's planned questions drop, that comparison is invalid: no verdict, not a
  pass, and the claim is not made.
- **Worst-case sensitivity.** Every gbrain-side drop is scored 0 and every server-side drop 100, and the four tests are
  run again. The result is reported beside the verdict. Whenever it disagrees with the verdict, the findings' first
  lines say so.
- **A missing re-read.** verdict.ts refuses to run when a row's particle-1 re-read is missing. Inside a re-read that
  ran, a question with no re-read answer is a harness drop, as above.

### Delivered-context gate

F6 reports each component row's fill rate (mean delivered cl100k tokens over the 8,000-token budget, `fill_rate` in
`rows.ndjson`) as a diagnostic. RS1 makes it a gate. A system row passes when its mean fill rate is at least 0.90. That is
the lower half of the memory-proof waves' 8,000 plus or minus 10% gate; the packer's cap makes the upper half hold by
construction. A row that fails the gate makes every comparison it enters descriptive: an estimate and an interval, no
verdict, not a pass. On F6's S2b the fill rates were 0.938 for gbrain arm P, 0.983 for the memory-bank server and 0.992
for the extract-first server.

### Receipts

verdict.ts refuses any receipt whose dataset revision is `unfilled`, or whose loaded files' SHA-256 differ from the
filled RS1 manifest the freeze records (`eval/decisions/datasets/rs1-manifest.json`, filled by the custodian). A later
fill only adds files and lists the earlier fill's SHA-256 under `earlier_fills`. This keeps stage 0's public-topic
conversations, which share RS1 slot names, out of any verdict.

### Secondaries

Only after the primary passes, in a fixed sequence (`eval/runner/stats/fixed-sequence.ts`), so alpha is not spent:

1. Superiority over the extract-first server: the lower bound is above 0 under both judge views.
2. Only if the first passes, superiority over the memory-bank server, under the same rule.

### Reported beside the verdict, not part of it

- **Bootstrap sensitivity.** The restricted wild cluster bootstrap-t (`eval/runner/stats/wild-cluster.ts`, Webb weights,
  9,999 draws) on the question-weighted estimate.
- **Per-reader and per-ability rows**, under each judge view.
- **The floor row**, and whether it sits below every system row under the protocol judge.
- **Each row's fill rate** and the gate result.
- **Dropped questions** by reason and side.
- **The first table:** cost per correct answer for claude-opus-5-5 and particle-1 first, then gpt-6.1-sol, from the
  metering proxy's usage fields. For each reader and row it is (the row's ingest, retrieval and background dollars
  divided by its questions, plus the reader's own dollars per answer) divided by that reader's graded accuracy under the
  protocol judge. Judge dollars are left out, because they buy the measurement, not the answer. A receipts directory
  without the cells' `runs/<cell>/meters.ndjson` reports the cost as missing rather than zero.
- **Null results.** A failed or invalid primary gets R48's "Why null?" cycle in custody before it is reported: exposure,
  task fit, power and saturation, and the bare model.

## Internal pilot

1. **Wave A.** The first 20 kept conversations run through every row, reader and judge.
2. **What leaves custody.** verdict.ts in pilot mode reads wave A receipts only and emits the four SDs of the
   per-conversation differences (two servers by two judge views) and their 4 by 4 correlation matrix. It emits no means,
   and a test checks that its output has no mean keys.
3. **Sizing.** power.ts sets N_required, the smallest N with simulated intersection-union power of at least 80% at true
   gaps of 0 and M = 3.5, and N_final = min(33, max(20, N_required)).
4. **Over the cap.** If N_required is above 33, the run pauses with `RS1_PILOT_OVER_CAP` (exit 3), reporting the margin
   reachable at 33 and the price of N_required, and GBEV-3 decides.
5. **Under the plan.** A lower SD stops the run earlier and saves money.

Resizing from the pilot's own SDs is Wittes and Brittain's internal pilot. Its known property, a small rise in type I
error above the nominal level, is accepted here rather than corrected. The means stay in custody, so the resize cannot
react to how gbrain is doing.

Wave B's cells are regenerated by a dated amendment commit once N_final is known.

## Power

These figures come from GBEV-48's model, which power.ts reproduces. F6's S2b gives the per-conversation SD at 10 items:
8.61 points under frontier judges and 5.92 under BEAM's judge for gbrain minus the memory-bank server, and 7.86 and 5.80
for the extract-first server. RS1 asks 20 items. The SD at 20 items follows SD20² = tau² + (SD10² - tau²) / 2, where tau
is the between-conversation SD of the true gap:

| Scenario | tau | SD at 20 items, frontier | SD at 20 items, BEAM judge |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Optimistic | 0 | 6.08 | 4.19 |
| **Central** | **3.2** | **6.49** | **4.76** |
| Pessimistic | 4.8 | 6.97 | 5.39 |
| Bound (no gain from 20 items) | | 8.61 | 5.92 |

All four tests must pass, at 80% power and one-sided alpha 0.05:

- **At 3.5 points:** 28 conversations central and 33 pessimistic at a true extract-first gap of 0; 24 and 27 at +2.
- **Correlation.** The assumed 0.5 correlation between the two comparisons moves N by at most 1 when set to 0.3.
- **At the bound** the design needs 46 conversations. That is why the pilot exists: at the bound, power at the planned N
  would be only 54 to 60%.
- **Type I error** at the boundary is 1.9% in simulation (40,000 runs), because the intersection-union rule is
  conservative.
- **Secondary power:** about 91% against the extract-first server if F6's +3.5 holds, and about 44% against the
  memory-bank server at +2.0. The second is not powered, and this file says so.

power.ts reproduces GBEV-48's analytic per-test n exactly and its joint intersection-union N within the Monte Carlo
tolerance power.ts states, with a seeded pseudo-random generator. Its tests use GBEV-48's `power.json` as a fixture.

pricing.ts exits 3 with `RS1_CHECKIN` when the projection passes $2,300. At stage 0's rates that happens for N_final of
about 30 or more, so a pilot that lands there brings a check-in with GBEV-3. pricing.ts runs again after stage 1
replaces the placeholder gbrain rates (F6's measured `f6-master` rates) with measured `rs1-stable` rates, and both
projections are recorded.

Every paid call runs in a GBEV-3 paid window. Ledger caps are runaway stops: hitting one pauses the run for an
extension and does not end the test.

## Error contract

Each stop and refusal pauses the run, and the case goes to GBEV-3.

| Code or refusal | Raised by | When |
|---|---|---|
| `RS1_STAGE1_OVER_CAP` | `bun eval/runner/q1/cell.ts plan --manifest rs1` | stage 1's estimate is above $60 |
| `RS1_PILOT_OVER_CAP` (exit 3) | verdict.ts pilot mode, through power.ts | N_required is above 33; it prints the margin reachable at 33 and the price of N_required |
| `RS1_CHECKIN` (exit 3) | pricing.ts | the check-in projection passes $2,300 |
| unfilled or mismatched manifest | the runner and verdict.ts | the RS1 manifest is unfilled, or a receipt's dataset revision is `unfilled` or its files' SHA-256 differ from the filled manifest |
| kept id outside the filled manifest | the runner | a cell names a conversation the filled manifest's `kept` list does not pin, or a unit's conversations are not all in it |
| missing particle-1 re-read | verdict.ts | a row has no particle-1 re-read |
| judge of the reader's own family, or particle-1 as judge | the cell definitions | a per-reader frontier plan breaks the family rule or names particle-1 |

The yield gate (2 or more drops in the first 5) also pauses minting and goes to GBEV-3.

## Risks

- **tau.** The 10 to 20 item SD scaling rests on tau = 3.2 from older dev pairs with a gemini reader and judge. The pilot
  measures the SD directly.
- **Yield.** 0.85 rests on one fixed-code conversation, c003, which passed 15 of 20 items on first pass. A yield of 0.65
  adds about $303. The yield gate catches a low yield early.
- **Voyage organization.** The opt-out is confirmed for garrytan's Organization only. No sealed text reaches Voyage until
  the custodian confirms the custody host's key belongs to it.
- **Custody host readiness.** mint.sh needs uv and Python 3.12 on the custody host, and the Gemini billing probe there
  has not run. Both are mint blockers.
- **The stable tag moving.** If the `stable` tag moves before the freeze merges, the new sha is re-read and pinned in
  this file before it freezes.
- **Minted versus BEAM's own conversations.** Minted chats may vary differently from BEAM's sealed ones. The pilot
  measures this too.

## What it can and can't claim

**If it passes:** on newly minted 1M-token conversations written by BEAM's generator from new topics, gbrain at the
stable tag (`27f8b9d6`), reading 8,000 tokens, is at most 3.5 points behind each of the two leading servers, the
memory-bank server and the extract-first server, under both BEAM's judge and frontier judges, read by Opus 5.5, GPT-6.1
Sol and particle-1. That puts gbrain in the leading group.

**It does not say:**

- that gbrain beats either server (that is the secondary, and only the extract-first one is powered);
- anything about how the two servers rank against each other;
- anything about other token budgets, other benchmarks, 10M-token histories or other gbrain pins.

The margin is wider than the 1.5 to 3.4-point spread among F6's top three external servers, so passing does not
separate gbrain from the next server down. Claims read "on new topics from BEAM's generator"; a second generator family
or a real-trace set is the next held-out step. A result favorable to particle-1 keeps its gap to Opus and the interval in
the headline.
