Week three of the rye starter and it is finally predictable. Feeding at a
one to five to five ratio every twenty four hours gives a clean peak at
around eight hours in a seventy two degree kitchen. The aroma has moved from
harsh acetone in week one to a mild yogurt tang, which the baking guide says
is the sign that the culture has settled into a stable mix of yeast and
lactic bacteria.

Switching from whole wheat to dark rye flour on day nine made the biggest
difference. Rye ferments visibly faster here, and the starter now doubles
reliably instead of stalling at half rise. Keeping the jar in the oven with
just the light on holds temperature within two degrees overnight.

Observations worth keeping:

- A stiffer mix at sixty percent hydration peaks slower but holds the peak
  for three hours, which is far more forgiving for weekday baking.
- Discard pancakes work best when the discard is no more than two days old.
- The first fully leavened loaf rose well but tasted flat because I cut the
  salt; going back to two percent salt fixed the flavor without slowing the
  final proof by any amount I could measure.

Next experiment is an overnight cold proof in the fridge to see whether the
crumb opens up without turning the loaf sour.
