title: "Drip irrigation overhaul for the back garden"
---

The drip system in the back garden has been losing pressure since early
spring. After walking the main line twice I found three splits in the half
inch tubing near the compost bins, all at points where the line crosses the
gravel path. Squirrels appear to be chewing the tubing where it sits exposed,
so burying those runs under two inches of mulch should stop the damage.

Replacing the splits took four compression couplings and about an hour. While
the line was open I flushed the whole loop and cleared the filter screen at
the manifold, which was half blocked with mineral scale. Static pressure at
the far emitter is back to fourteen psi, which matches the reading from when
the system was first installed.

Remaining work for next weekend:

- Swap the four oldest emitters on the tomato bed for pressure compensating
  ones so the slope stops starving the last two plants.
- Add a flush valve at the low corner near the fig tree.
- Put a fresh battery in the timer and shift the morning cycle from six to
  five so watering finishes before the sun hits the beds.

Total cost so far is under thirty dollars in parts, far cheaper than the
quote for trenching a new line, and the beds are already looking less
stressed after one full week on the repaired loop.
